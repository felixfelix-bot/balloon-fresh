## 2. SUBSTANCE — **CONFIRM, with qualifications**

The visible logic is broadly self-consistent with the proposed balanced station:

- A roughly **15 dBi 2.4 GHz antenna** is shown at about **29° HPBW**, which plausibly supports a light or open-loop positioner rather than a precision closed-loop tracker.
- The **27 dBi case**, at roughly **7.3°**, is correctly presented as requiring substantially better pointing and as being associated with an expensive commercial tracker.
- The cost comparison supports using a **1 W PA plus a moderate-gain 2.4 GHz Yagi** rather than buying much more antenna gain. The chart’s PA and antenna figures are directionally consistent with that trade.
- On receive, the figure correctly emphasizes that an LNA is not equivalent to adding antenna gain: the LNA is after the antenna, and its useful system improvement is limited by noise figure, cable loss, receiver noise, and cascade behavior. The stated **~6.4 dB effective improvement** is therefore much more credible than treating a nominal 20 dB LNA as 20 dB of link improvement.
- The proposed **14.8 dBi 433 MHz receive Yagi** is also consistent with the note that antenna gain is preferable below roughly 15 dBi, assuming the stated prices and system-noise assumptions.
- The approximate **EUR1032** balanced-station recommendation is therefore supported in principle: spend on useful receive antenna gain, use moderate 2.4 GHz transmit gain with PA power, and avoid the cost and pointing burden of a high-gain dish or precision tracker.

However, a few claims are stronger than the figure alone establishes:

- The **2.4 GHz beamwidth curve cannot directly determine the pointing requirement of the 433 MHz Yagi**. Beamwidth depends on physical aperture and antenna design, not gain alone; the two bands should not be conflated.
- “**Open-loop**” is plausible for 15 dBi/29°, but not guaranteed solely from HPBW. Balloon angular rate, wind-induced motion, positioner accuracy, link margin, and required availability also matter.
- “**A PA can replace antenna gain**” is valid in an EIRP/link-budget sense, but only within regulatory EIRP limits, PA linearity and thermal limits, feed-loss assumptions, and the actual transmitter operating mode.
- The LNA conclusion is properly qualified by the cascade cap, but the exact **6.4 dB** is system-specific rather than universal.

Thus the recommendation is **CONFIRMED as a reasonable balanced design**, while the tracker-class and cost-per-dB conclusions should be presented as approximate, assumption-dependent comparisons rather than universal rules.

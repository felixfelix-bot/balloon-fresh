## 0. The question this record answers

The operator asked: *"How do we deal with the SX1280?"* — meaning, in the context of
the drift work already landed (ADR-042, ADR-056) and running (the FLRC and NTC sibling
records), what provision, if any, the **retained SX1280** needs against temperature
drift.

This record answers it in the operator's own order of priorities:

1. **What the SX1280 is for**, and **whether its assigned role needs drift control at
   all** — established first, from primary sources, because if it does not, the correct
   provision is the cheapest one available and it is the one that already exists.
2. **If drift does matter**, the ranked options.
3. **What it costs.**
4. **What must not be proposed** (a heater or an OCXO).

## 1. What the SX1280 is for (and the superseded-ban correction)

### 1.1 It is retained, by operator decision, as a dedicated 2.4 GHz ranging radio

ADR-029's header carries the amendment verbatim:

> - Status: **Proposed** — the *design direction* this ADR records was ratified by the
>   operator on 2026-10-04; the *text* has not been accepted by a human, so it does not
>   say Accepted.
> - **Amended 2026-10-05 (operator decision, see D2b):** the SX1280 is **retained** for
>   ranging and the MAX-M10S is confirmed.

ADR-029 **D2b** records the decision and its two independent grounds: *ranging* — the
F33's in-module RTToF is a *different implementation* whose on-air compatibility with
the **existing SX1280 fleet** (the E28 modules, the `hub_board_v1_placed` SX1280, the
ranging firmware already written for them) is not established, so a radio that ranges
only against itself does not replace one that ranges against the ground segment already
in the field — and **JLC SMT-placeability**.

### 1.2 There is no live SX1280 ban — the ban record was written from a wrong diagnosis

This is restated here so nobody re-litigates it.

- `docs/adr/101-lr2021-only-ban-sx1280.md` — the file whose *filename* says
  "ban sx1280". Its own Status header reads:

  > ## Status
  > **SUPERSEDED by ADR-020** (2026-07-23)
  >
  > This ADR was originally written with incorrect diagnosis.

  and its body states the mechanism: *"A diagnostic using SX1280 1-byte opcodes (0xC0)
  was sent to an LR2021 chip. The chip returned garbage (0x21) because it doesn't
  understand SX1280 protocol. This was misinterpreted as \"chip responds to SX1280.\""*

- `docs/adr/020-deprecate-radiolib-adopt-raw-lr2021-spi.md` — its Status header reads:

  > ## Status
  > Accepted (2026-07-23)
  > **Supersedes ADR-101** (which incorrectly banned SX1280 and mandated RadioLib).

- ADR-035 already states the same conclusion, so this is not a new finding:
  *"ADR-101 `101-lr2021-only-ban-sx1280.md`: \"SUPERSEDED by ADR-020\" — no live SX1280
  ban exists."*

**Net: the SX1280 is neither banned nor superseded. It is retained, and what remains
open is only its reference (clock) choice.**

### 1.3 Present in the v9 schematic, on SPI3, with no reference part drawn yet

The SX1280 is real in the board of record: `v9_flight.kicad_sch` uses
`lib_id "balloon_flight_v9:SX1280"` (value `SX1280`, footprint
`balloon_flight_v9:SX1280_QFN24`) and carries the nets `SX1280_SCK`, `SX1280_MOSI`,
`SX1280_MISO`, `SX1280_CS_N`, `SX1280_BUSY`, `SX1280_RESET_N`, `SX1280_DIO1`,
`SX1280_DIO2`, `SX1280_DIO3`, `SX1280_ANT_SW` (34 occurrences of the token `SX1280` in
the sheet). The GPIO plan is ADR-108 (SPI3 on GPIO14–17; BUSY 38; RESET 39; DIO1 40;
DIO3 41; ANT_SW 42).

Two facts about the schematic matter for this decision and are stated rather than
implied:

- **No reference (crystal **or** TCXO) is drawn for the SX1280 anywhere in the v9
  schematic.** The SX1280 symbol's pin list is
  `1 VDD_IN, 2 VDD_IO, 3 GND, 4 NRESET, 5 BUSY, 6 NSS, 7 SCK, 8 MOSI, 9 MISO,
  10 DIO1, 11 DIO2, 12 DIO3, 13 ANT_SW, 14 RFIO, 15–24 GND` — there is no `XTA`/`XTB`
  pin on the symbol at all. The reference is therefore an *undrawn schematic slot*.
- **Every SX1280 pad number is a declared-unverified placeholder.** The symbol's own
  Description reads: *"Semtech SX1280 2.4 GHz ranging transceiver. \*\*\* PIN NUMBERING
  AND LAND PATTERN NOT VERIFIED - Semtech datasheet unreachable on this host; see
  TODO(unverified) notes on the sheet. \*\*\*"*, and the sheet's `OPEN-13` item repeats
  it. This record inherits that gap (§8).

### 1.4 The assigned role is **ranging only** — not a link, not a time reference

ADR-034 re-points the vehicle's 2.4 GHz **link** at a separate bare `LoRa2021`
(433 MHz TX on the F33), and ADR-035's slot table gives the SX1280 exactly one slot:

> | Role | Part | Band | Contends for a slot? |
> | TX | `LoRa2021F33-2G4` | 433 MHz | **yes** — TX slot |
> | RX | bare `LoRa2021` | 2.4 GHz | **yes** — RX slot (receive-arm window) |
> | Ranging | `SX1280` | 2.4 GHz | **yes** — ranging slot (bidirectional) |

So the SX1280 is **not** a link carrier and **not** the vehicle's time/frequency
authority — that authority is GPS UTC/1PPS (ADR-017/019/021/023; restated by ADR-056
§D3, which makes 1PPS discipline the primary provision "because it costs zero grams and
zero watts").

## 2. Does the ranging role require drift control? — the first and most important answer

**Finding: on the evidence available, drift control for the SX1280's ranging role is
NOT established as required.** The reasoning, each step from a primary source:

### 2.1 The ranging engine is a self-referenced, self-calibrating two-way measurement

The vendored RadioLib SX1280 ranging path (the driver this project already uses for the
E28/T3S3 ranging fleet, `firmware/esp32-e28-range/`) is a **master/slave two-way
exchange**:

- `startRanging()` puts the chip in `RADIOLIB_SX128X_PACKET_TYPE_RANGING`, sets the role
  (`RADIOLIB_SX128X_RANGING_ROLE_MASTER` then `setTx`, or `..._SLAVE` then `setRx`), and
  writes a **per-bandwidth/spreading-factor calibration word** to
  `RADIOLIB_SX128X_REG_RANGING_CALIBRATION_MSB`, with the source line
  `// this is the default calibration from AN1200.29` (`SX1280.cpp`).
- The distance is read back from the chip's own result registers and scaled by its own
  bandwidth:
  `float SX1280::getRangingResult() { int32_t raw = getRangingResultCommon(false); return((float)raw * 150.0f / (4.096f * this->bandwidthKhz)); }`
  reading `RADIOLIB_SX128X_REG_RANGING_RESULT_MSB/MID/LSB` (0x0961–0x0963).

So the number the vehicle gets is the chip's own round-trip timing measurement, scaled
by the chip's own configured bandwidth. Nothing in that computation consumes a
**pre-shared absolute frequency** between the two ends; the calibration is
self-applied per BW/SF.

### 2.2 The SX1280 reports its own carrier offset, valid in ranging mode

The same driver exposes `getFrequencyError()`, which reads `FEI_MSB/MID/LSB`
(0x0954–0x0956) and **returns a value for the ranging modem as well as LoRa**:

```cpp
uint8_t modem = getPacketType();
if(!((modem == RADIOLIB_SX128X_PACKET_TYPE_LORA) || (modem == RADIOLIB_SX128X_PACKET_TYPE_RANGING))) {
  return(0.0);
}
```

That is the driver's own evidence that the SX1280 can **measure** its carrier offset in
ranging mode. It is *not* used by the ranging-result path above — it is a separate
readout the application may use — but it means a host-side correction input exists at
zero added mass if one is ever wanted.

### 2.3 The role is not flight-critical in the way the drift records mean

ADR-042 §D5's conservative default ("until the number is measured, prefer the fix that
removes the drift question from the **flight-critical path**") is written for the **link**
that must demodulate, and ADR-056 §D2's TCXO requirement is written for "whenever the
SX1280 **is a flight reference**". Neither trigger is met here: per §1.4 the SX1280
carries ranging only, and the link and the timing authority live elsewhere. So the
standing records impose **no** TCXO obligation on this part.

### 2.4 The repo's *quantified* tolerances are link tolerances, and they are large

`docs/analysis/external-clock-and-ocxo.md` §C1 records the datasheet carrier-offset
tolerances — and they are **link** figures, not ranging figures:

- LoRa `LORA_FERR_L1` = **±25 % BW**, all bandwidths; `LORA_FERR_L2` = **±33 % BW**.
- FLRC at 2.4 GHz: **±150 kHz** (2.6 Mbps), ±100 kHz (1.3/1.04 Mbps), ±70 kHz
  (650 kbps), ±50 kHz (520 kbps).

Against those, a plain `±20–30 ppm` crystal at 2.4 GHz is `48–72 kHz`, i.e. inside the
LoRa bands and inside all but the two slowest FLRC rates. **These numbers apply to the
2.4 GHz link, which is on the bare `LoRa2021`/F33 — not to the SX1280's ranging engine.
They are cited here only to show the drift headroom the *project* already relies on, and
they must not be transferred to the ranging result.**

### 2.5 The nearest ranging-accuracy primary source in the repo is about the LR2021, not the SX1280

The only ranging-accuracy statement committed anywhere in this repo is the Semtech
datasheet v2.2 known-limitations item, and it names a *different* engine:

> **22.2 RTTOF Accuracy for SX1280-compatible Bandwidths (LR2021/LR2022)**
> For LoRa bandwidths 203, 406 and 812 kHz (at either 2.4 GHz or sub-GHz), the RTToF
> accuracy is optimized when calling specific workarounds described in
> `lr20xx_workarounds.c`. Refer to the LR20xx driver for reference.
> — `docs/lr2021-research/errata/semtech-datasheet-v2.2-section22-known-limitations-and-workarounds.txt`

That is the **LR2021's** RTToF workaround, not the SX1280's ranging engine. The SX1280's
own datasheet-stated ranging accuracy **and** its sensitivity to carrier offset are
**not** in this repo and the Semtech datasheet is not reachable from this host (§8).

### 2.6 Conclusion of §2, and the measurement that would settle it

**A two-way (round-trip) measurement uses one clock for both legs, so the first-order
error a one-way link suffers from a clock offset is not what limits it; the residual
sensitivity is a property of the chip's own ranging engine and calibration, which the
repo does not quantify.** On the available evidence the SX1280 does **not** need a
compensated reference to do its job — which is the honest half-answer; the other half is
that this is **unproven**, so it is recorded as a named bench test rather than asserted:

> **THE MEASUREMENT THAT SETTLES §2 (named, not run — design work only).**
> Against a **fixed, tape-measured bench separation**, with the SX1280 references at
> their fitted hardware:
> 1. cold-soak both ends at **−40 / −20 / 0 / +20 / +40 °C** (the LR2021 reference's own
>    characterised window, ADR-042 §A5) and, at each point, run `N` master/slave ranging
>    exchanges at the operational point (**2440 MHz, BW 812.5 kHz** — the E28 console's
>    ranging BW), logging `getRangingResult()` against the tape distance;
> 2. log `getFrequencyError()` per exchange to record the **actual** carrier offset in
>    ppm at each temperature;
> 3. **Pass** = the ranging distance error stays inside the mission's required ranging
>    accuracy across the whole sweep (and the offset stays inside whatever the ranging
>    engine tolerates). **If it passes, no TCXO is needed and option (a) stays unpopulated.
>    If it fails, populate option (a).**

## 3. If drift does matter — the ranked provisions

### (a) An external TCXO on XTA pin 4, gated by an **external load switch**

**Feasibility is verified, and the on-chip gate does not exist.** From
`docs/analysis/external-clock-and-ocxo.md` §A3 (read against Semtech
**DS.SX1280-1.W.APP Rev 1.0**):

- The SX1280 is a **bare transceiver IC that needs an external reference**. Pin table:
  **pin 4 = `XTA` — "Reference oscillator connection or TCXO input"**; **pin 6 = `XTB` —
  "Reference oscillator connection"**. `FXOSC` = **52 MHz** nominal (§3.7 / Table 3-9).
- **§14.2 "Application Design with optional TCXO"** (p. 124) with **Figure 14-3
  "Application Schematic with Optional TCXO"**: the TCXO is **AC-coupled into XTA**.
- **There is no on-chip TCXO-enable.** In Figure 14-3 the TCXO has **its own rail**
  (VDD_3V3 through ferrite beads) and **DIO3 is simply pulled to VDD_RADIO through a 0 Ω
  resistor (R15)** — an ordinary multi-purpose I/O (pin table: *"pin 10 = DIO3 — Optional
  multi-purpose digital I/O"*), **not** a TCXO power switch. Therefore **gating a TCXO
  supply requires an external load switch** (driven by an MCU GPIO).

**Because it draws real current it MUST be gatable.** Using the repo's own figures: the
LR2021's TCXO-regulator load current is **`ILTCXO` 1.5 mA typ / 4 mA max** = **4.9–13.2 mW
at 3.3 V**; at the repo's **~1 % TX duty** that is **≈50–130 µW averaged**, and **0 µW at
night** because TX is daylight-only (ADR-036). Compared with the **100 µW night anchor**
(ADR-036), a **continuously-on** TCXO would be **≈49–132×** the anchor — hence the load
switch: gated, the night contribution is **zero**, not "a little". `TODO(unverified)`:
`ILTCXO` is the **LR2021's** figure and is an analogue, not the SX1280's; the SX1280's own
TCXO drive level is not stated in the datasheet read here (§8).

**Settling time.** A TCXO is a crystal plus a compensation network whose output is
correct **within milliseconds of power-up** (contrast an oven, §6), so it only has to be
powered while the radio is on: switch the rail on and let it settle **before** the
ranging/TX window opens. The exact settle time is **`TODO(unverified)`** — take it from
the chosen TCXO's datasheet, not from this record.

### (b) Restrict the SX1280 to roles where drift is immaterial, and keep timing-critical work on the LR2021 (which has the XOSC-adjacent sensor and the NTC path)

**Status: already satisfied — this is the current state, not an action.** ADR-034 moved
the 2.4 GHz **link** off the SX1280 onto a bare `LoRa2021`; ADR-035 gives the SX1280
**only** the ranging slot; and the vehicle's timing authority is GPS 1PPS, not any radio
(§1.4, §2.3). The LR2021 is the chip that carries the XOSC-adjacent temperature sensor
and the `SetTempCompCfg`/`SetNtcParams` path (ADR-042 Addendum A) — and none of that is
transferable to the SX1280.

### (c) Reject the NTC route for the SX1280 outright

**`SetTempCompCfg` (0x0132) and `SetNtcParams` (0x0133) are LR2021 commands.** They are
in no way available on the SX1280, and the vendored RadioLib SX128x driver wraps **no
temperature command at all**:

- the driver's command table contains no `GetTemp`, no `SetTempComp`, no TCXO-enable —
  and, whereas the vendored `SX126x` module takes a `float tcxoVoltage` argument in
  `begin()` and carries a `tcxoDelay`, `grep -i tcxo` over `src/modules/SX128x/` returns
  **nothing**;
- **`0x9A` is `RADIOLIB_SX128X_CMD_SET_ADVANCED_RANGING`**, not a temperature command
  (`SX128x.h:57`), and `0x97` is `RADIOLIB_SX128X_CMD_CLEAR_IRQ_STATUS` (`SX128x.h:48`).

So the on-chip/NTC compensation route **does not exist** for this part and is rejected.
(Whether the SX1280 *silicon* has any internal temperature sensor is **`TODO(unverified)`** —
§8.)

## 4. The chosen provision

**Chosen: do nothing to the SX1280's reference by default — fly it on a plain,
uncompensated 52 MHz-class reference, add no TCXO, and gate no rail — and hold option (a)
live as the reserved fallback by keeping the reference a schematic decision (it still is,
§1.3).**

Rationale, in order:

1. **The role does not require it on the evidence** (§2): ranging is a self-referenced,
   self-calibrating two-way measurement; the chip additionally reports its own carrier
   offset valid in ranging mode; the part is not the link and not the time authority; and
   the standing TCXO obligations (ADR-042 §D5, ADR-056 §D2) are not triggered because the
   SX1280 is not a flight reference.
2. **The cheapest fix is the one that already exists.** No new part, no rail, no gating
   logic, no settle-time budget, no current to duty-cycle — it is the reference the
   bench/standalone SX1280 boards already use.
3. **The conservative-default rule is satisfied without a purchase.** ADR-042 §D5 says
   the decision cannot be final until the offset-tolerance number is known. Here the
   instrument that produces the number is a **bench test** (§2.6), not a part, so the
   honest default is to *measure first* — and because there is no v9 PCB (§5) the fix, if
   needed, costs **no respin**.
4. **The option is not lost.** Option (a) is verified feasible (§3a) and the pin plan
   already breaks out spare SX1280 control I/O (`SX1280_DIO2`, `SX1280_DIO3`,
   `SX1280_ANT_SW` — ADR-108), so a TCXO-rail load-switch GPIO is available if the bench
   test flips the decision.

**Explicitly not chosen now:** no TCXO populated, no load switch populated, no gate. If
§2.6's pass criterion is missed, option (a) is populated and this record is amended —
which is a schematic edit, not a board respin.

## 5. What it costs

**Cost of the chosen provision today: zero mass, zero power, zero respin.** It is a
decision to leave the SX1280's reference as the uncompensated crystal and to keep the
reference slot open — not a part.

**Cost of option (a) if it is ever populated** (the tens-of-milligrams decision, not a
mass problem):

- **Mass — `ESTIMATE`.** A plain 52 MHz TCXO (3.2 × 2.5 mm or 2.5 × 2.0 mm class ceramic),
  a load switch (SOT-23-5 or SC70), plus an AC-coupling cap and decoupling. By the repo's
  own package-mass analogues (`docs/PAYLOAD-WEIGHT-ESTIMATES.md`: SOT-23-5 = **40 mg**,
  1206 = 30 mg, 0805 = 15 mg, 0402 = **2.5 mg**; the whole V1 SMD set = **137.5 mg**), this
  is a **tens-of-milligrams** item. `TODO(unverified)`: no TCXO or load-switch *unit mass*
  is committed in the repo, so the sum is an estimate by package analogue, not a cited
  figure. It is immaterial at the repo's grams resolution (targets **< 9 g** minimal /
  **< 14 g** mesh).
- **Power — `4.9–13.2 mW` while on** (`ILTCXO` analogue, §3a), **≈50–130 µW averaged** at
  the repo's ~1 % duty, **0 µW at night** (TX is daylight-only, ADR-036). Gated, so it
  never touches the 100 µW night anchor.
- **Respin cost: zero, and this can be stated cleanly.** **There is no v9 PCB.** The only
  `v9` `.kicad_pcb` in the repo is the **wing** (`tracker/hardware/wing_board/wing_board_v9.kicad_pcb`);
  **no** `.kicad_pcb` anywhere references `SX1280`; the v9 flight/hub board exists only as
  a **schematic** (`v9_flight.kicad_sch`), and ADR-048 §5 item 4 records the same for the
  hub side — *"**No v9 hub PCB exists**, so this cannot be measured yet."* The SX1280's
  reference is therefore still a **schematic decision**, and changing it costs **no board
  respin**.

## 6. What must not be proposed: a heater, or an OCXO

**Rejected, on arithmetic already done** — reaffirmed here, not re-derived.
`docs/analysis/external-clock-and-ocxo.md` Part B scales two real datasheet parts to the
cold end (SiTime `SiT5501` ≈ **0.34 W** cold; Raltron `OXD30` ≈ **5.2 W** cold) against the
**33.264 J** usable bank (ADR-047 §3.2 / ADR-051 / ADR-054), giving a bank life of
**≈6–98 s**, and against the **100 µW night anchor** a factor of **≈3 400×–52 000×**. The
structural objection is decisive: **an oven holds its crystal warm *continuously* and
cannot be switched off** (`SiT5501` `OE`-disable saves 43 → 43 mA, i.e. nothing), whereas
a TCXO is correct within milliseconds of power-up and so can be gated. This is the same
verdict ADR-042 §D1 (board heater), ADR-043 (cold-qualification heating, Accepted) and
ADR-056 §D1 already reached. **The heater/OCXO branch is closed; do not reopen it.**

## 7. Relationship to standing decisions

### Adopted / consistent (no edit to any of them)

- **ADR-029** (Proposed) — D2b's retention of the SX1280 is taken as given; this record
  decides only the reference. ADR-029 is not edited.
- **ADR-034, ADR-035** (Proposed) — consistent: ADR-035's slot table is what makes the
  SX1280 ranging-only (§1.4).
- **ADR-056** (Proposed) — **§D2 is scoped, not overridden.** Its requirement reads
  *"require a TCXO-bearing module **whenever the SX1280 is a flight reference**"*; this
  record states that the SX1280 is **not** a flight reference (ranging-only, §1.4/§2.3),
  so D2 imposes no TCXO here. ADR-056 §D3 (GPS 1PPS discipline, zero grams / zero watts)
  is the timing provision and **stands**. ADR-056 is not edited.
- **ADR-101** — the SX1280 "ban" file; **SUPERSEDED by ADR-020**, quoted in §1.2. No live
  ban exists and this record adds nothing to that beyond restating it for readers.
- **ADR-043** (Accepted) — heating rejected; respected.

### Extended (stated; the parent record is not edited)

- **ADR-042 §D5** — the carrier-offset-tolerance gate ("**Δf_max = (measured carrier drift)
  − (offset tolerance)**; if Δf_max ≤ 0, no mitigation is needed") is the right gate and
  is **extended** by this record with the **ranging-specific** version of it: what is
  unmeasured here is the *ranging result's* tolerance, not the link's. ADR-042 §D1 (heater
  rejected) and §D2 (TCXO first) are reaffirmed, not superseded.

### Evidence base, and a stated departure

- `docs/analysis/external-clock-and-ocxo.md` recommends an **unheated, gated TCXO on
  XTA (pin 4)** for the SX1280 ranging path (§"Recommendation" item 2). That
  recommendation is **conditional** — *"If a bare-crystal radio must be flown (SX1280
  ranging path)"* — and it does not establish that the ranging role needs one. This record
  grades that recommendation against the operator's first question ("does it need it at
  all?") and, finding the need unproven, **defers the populate decision to the §2.6 bench
  test** rather than adopting it unconditionally. The departure is stated so a reader who

## 9. Consequences

### Positive

- The operator's question is answered **in his own order**: what the part is for, whether
  the role needs drift control (**not established as required**), then the ranked
  provisions — so no future reader reaches for a TCXO before asking whether ranging needs
  one.
- The **superseded-ban correction** (§1.2) is quoted from the actual Status lines, so the
  question is not re-litigated a fourth time.
- The **NTC route is closed for this part** (§3c) with the driver evidence, so nobody
  proposes it again.
- The chosen provision costs **zero mass, zero power, zero respin**, and the graded
  fallback is **verified feasible** and costs none either — the decision is cheap to hold
  and cheap to reverse.
- The heater/OCXO branch is restated as closed (§6) with its arithmetic, so it is not
  reopened.

### Costs (accepted)

- **One bench campaign is owed** (§2.6) before the reference is frozen — a cold sweep
  with a tape-measured separation and a `getFrequencyError()` log. Until it runs, the
  "no TCXO" choice rests on mechanism plus the absence of a contrary figure, not on a
  measured ranging tolerance.
- **The SX1280's reference slot stays open** in the schematic until that campaign lands,
  which is deliberate (it is what makes the fallback free) but does mean the v9 schematic
  is not yet complete on this point.

## 10. Notes

- **Why one record and not two.** "What the SX1280 is for", "does ranging need drift
  control", and "which provision" are one decision — *how this vehicle keeps the ranging
  radio honest in the cold* — and they come from one evidence base. Splitting them would
  let a reader take the provision without the finding.
- **Why the answer is `do nothing`, stated plainly.** Because the first question the
  operator asked has a real answer that is not "buy a better oscillator": on the evidence,
  the ranging engine does not depend on the offset the way a link does, the part is not a
  flight reference, and the number that would prove it is a bench measurement rather than
  a purchase. The cheapest fix is the one that already exists.
- **What would falsify §4.** A §2.6 bench run whose ranging error leaves the mission
  requirement as the reference drifts — that populates option (a) by amendment, and it is
  the one thing that would do so. (A §2.6 run that *passes* would let the reserved
  provision be dropped from the schematic entirely.)
- **Design work only.** No part, board, or test is authorised or ordered by this record.

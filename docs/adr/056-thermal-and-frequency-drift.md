## 0. The question this record answers

The operator asked, verbatim intent:

> *"Do you already have a heaters provision for the oscillator of the radio so that we don't have
> frequency drift? If not, please include that in the plan as well."*

The answer is **no heater provision exists, and none should be added for oscillator drift**. This
record fixes the provision that replaces it and records the thermal strategy in the same place.
It **does not** re-open ADR-042 (heater rejection + TCXO-first) or ADR-043 (cold-qualification
heating rejection); it **extends** ADR-042 §D3 and adds the two things ADR-042 does not carry — a
**per-radio reference census** and **GPS 1PPS discipline**.

---

## 1. Context — the evidence, in brief

All of the following is established with arithmetic in
`docs/analysis/thermal-and-frequency-drift.md`; only the load-bearing numbers are restated here.

1. **The F33-2G4 already carries a 0.5 ppm built-in TCXO** (`docs/V9-RADIO-SITE-MATRIX.md` §2;
   `docs/LR2021-LESSONS-2026-09.md` line 23). `docs/F33-MODULE-PLAN.md` line 115 records that the
   VTCXO decoupling cap **C3 was removed** because the TCXO is internal — the design already banked it.
2. **The bare LoRa2021 — which is the DEFAULT flight radio at both v9 sites — has a crystal, no TCXO
   and no NTC** (`docs/V9-RADIO-SITE-MATRIX.md` §2, §6; open item O2). This is the real drift gap.
3. **The SX1280's reference is a 52 MHz module-fitted TCXO on the E80 kit but a bare 52 MHz crystal
   on the custom standalone boards** (`docs/FLRC-RANGE-DUAL-PLATFORM-ACTION-PLAN.md` lines 29, 58,
   103; `firmware/rp2040/src/bench_radio_sx1280.cpp:59`).
4. **A MAX-M10S is on the board with its 1PPS routed** (`docs/adr/108-f33-sx1280-pin-plan.md:38`,
   `GNSS_PPS`), and four accepted ADRs already make GPS UTC the timing authority (ADR-017/019/021/023).
5. **The OCXO-class heater arithmetic fails at every plausible thermal conductance.** With
   `P_heater = G · ΔT`, a turning-point setpoint of ≈ +25 °C against a −50…−56 °C ambient
   (ΔT = 80 K):

   | G | `P_heater` | × the 0.388 W daylight average | × the 100 µW night anchor | × the 33.264 J bank (10 h night) |
   |---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | 3 mW/K `ESTIMATE` | 0.240 W | 0.62× | — | **260×** |
   | **10 mW/K (ADR-042 §D1's own `R_thermal ≈ 100 K/W`)** `ESTIMATE` | **0.800 W** | **2.06×** | **8000×** | **866×** |
   | 30 mW/K `ESTIMATE` | 2.400 W | 6.19× | — | **2597×** |
   | 100 mW/K `ESTIMATE` | 8.000 W | 20.6× | — | **8658×** |

   Even the most insulated estimate (G = 3 mW/K) drains the **entire 33.264 J usable bank**
   (ADR-047 §3.2) in **~139 s**.
6. **The adjacent needs are not heating problems either.** Holding the electronics at −40 °C to
   rescue the −40 °C-rated parts costs **0.160 W = 41 % of the daylight average** (G = 10 mW/K) and
   **173× the bank** over a 10 h night; keeping −20 °C costs **0.360 W = 93 %** of the average.
   Supercap cold ESR/capacitance is a characterisation problem (but with an ESR-rise bound worth
   watching); cell cracking is thermal contraction whose fix is ADR-052's **end-only unbonded mount**.
7. **The in-flight `adr/thermal-drift-strategy` work is already on `main`** (merged by `d33b29c`;
   branch tip `da3ba7c`; tag `backup/adr-thermal-drift-strategy-20261007`). It produced **ADR-042**
   and **ADR-043**, both on `main`. Nothing was discarded; this record builds on them.

## 2. Decision

### D1 — No heater provision for oscillator drift (reaffirms ADR-042 §D1; the arithmetic is re-derived here in G form)

**Rejected.** An OCXO-class heater that holds a crystal at its turning point fails by **2.06×**
against the array's 0.388 W daylight average, **8000×** against the 100 µW night anchor, and
**866×** against the entire 33.264 J usable supercapacitor bank — at the repo's own assumed thermal
resistance — and it fails at **every** plausible `G` (§1.5). ADR-042 §D1's rejection is
**reaffirmed, not superseded**; this record only restates the same law as `P = G·ΔT` and measures it
against the 33.264 J bank (33.264 / 0.800 = 41.6 s) that ADR-042 did not have to hand. The
structural objections also stand: a heat-only loop pays for the **lift**, not the setpoint; a
*controlled* loop needs the MCU awake, against ADR-036; and a board heater warms the **MS5611**,
corrupting altitude telemetry (ADR-042 §D6).

**The heater that already exists at the right scale is the one inside a TCXO** — it holds a
crystal's micro-thermal mass, not the whole board.

### D2 — Oscillator reference standard: every radio that flies as a reference must be TCXO-bearing or GPS-disciplined

**Decision:** *no radio may be relied on as a flight frequency/time reference unless it has a
compensated reference (TCXO) or is disciplined by GPS 1PPS (D3).* Concretely:

- **`LoRa2021F33-2G4`** — carries a **0.5 ppm built-in TCXO**. **No change.** Prefer it wherever the
  link needs it (already the reason `docs/V9-RADIO-SITE-MATRIX.md` §6 gives for wanting the HP leg).
- **bare `LoRa2021`** (the default flight radio) — crystal only. Acceptable **only** if it is
  disciplined (D3). Where discipline is unavailable, the compensated options are (a) fly the F33,
  or (b) close the LR2021's own on-chip loop with a **100 K NTC on pin 3** + series R to the VTCXO
  rail (`SetTempCompCfg 0x0132`, ADR-042 Addendum A2). Marked `TODO(unverified)`: whether pin 3 is
  broken out on the NiceRF module's castellations.
- **`SX1280`** — **require a TCXO-bearing module** whenever the SX1280 is a flight reference. A bare
  52 MHz crystal (the custom-standalone case) is uncompensated. `TODO(unverified)`: the selected
  SX1280 module's TCXO control pin.
- **`ESP32-S3`** — its own clock does **not** set the RF carrier; no TCXO required for drift. Its
  self-heating is a *siting* aid (D4), not a compensation.
- **`MAX-M10S`** — the reference/authority; supplies 1PPS. No action.

### D3 — GPS 1PPS discipline is the PRIMARY provision (extends ADR-042 §D3)

**Decision:** the vehicle's residual radio-frequency error is **measured against the MAX-M10S 1PPS
and corrected in firmware**, and this is the primary provision because it costs **zero grams and
zero watts** — the GNSS receiver is already drawing its tracking current for position/telemetry, and
the 1PPS pin is already routed to GPIO21.

Mechanism, recorded as the requirement:

1. **Measure** `Δf/f = (N_measured − f_ref·1s) / (f_ref·1s)` by counting the radio reference between
   consecutive 1PPS edges.
2. **Average** over an `M`-second window. Counting resolution `1/(f_ref·M)`:
   **0.0031 ppm** at 32 MHz / **0.0019 ppm** at 52 MHz for `M = 10 s`; **0.00031 / 0.00019 ppm** for
   `M = 100 s`. The practical limit is 1PPS edge noise σ_t, which averages as `σ_t/(1 s·√M)`.
3. **Correct** by writing the measured `Δf/f` into the synthesizer as
   `f_set = f_nominal − Δf` — the same correction ADR-042 §D3 specifies, but **measured** instead of
   read from a pre-characterised `f(T)` table. The synthesizer can represent it: one LSB of the
   18-bit divider (`frf = f_rf·2^18/f_xtal`) is **0.2819 ppm at 433 MHz / 0.0509 ppm at 2.4 GHz**
   for the LR2021 (32 MHz), and **0.4581 / 0.0827 ppm** for the SX1280 (52 MHz).

**Achievable correction (target):** bring a bare-crystal radio's residual frequency error from the
uncompensated cold-soak figure to the **0.01–0.1 ppm order within a 10–100 s window**, at zero mass
and zero added power. The exact figure is gated on the measured `σ_t` (`TODO(unverified)`) and on
ADR-042 §D5's carrier-offset-tolerance number.

**Consistency with the existing sync ADRs — no amendment needed.** ADR-017, ADR-019 (which already
wires PPS→GP14 for "microsecond accuracy"), ADR-021 and ADR-023 all make GPS UTC the **schedule**
authority; none constrains the **radio carrier frequency**. Applying 1PPS to the carrier is
consistent and additive. The **only** relationship that is an amendment is to **ADR-042 §D3**, which
this record **extends** (a measured, table-free path to the same correction); ADR-042 §D1 and §D2 are
untouched. The relationship is stated here; ADR-042 is not edited.

### D4 — Passive mitigation is a ranked SECONDARY measure, never the fix, and never by bonding the cells

**Decision:** enclosure insulation, added thermal mass, and **siting the reference near a
self-heating node** (the ESP32-S3) are accepted as secondary helps only. They cannot hold +25 °C and
so cannot replace D2/D3. Two hard constraints:

- ADR-043 already rules insulation out as the *night* fix (night draws µA), and **cold is beneficial
  to the RF front end, the array, and the pressure sensor** — so passive measures must not fight that.
- Passive mitigation must be applied to the **electronics enclosure only**. It must **never** be
  achieved by bonding the solar cells: ADR-052 decides **end-only, no adhesive bond, cells free to
  move under thermal cycling**, and that decision stands.

### D5 — The adjacent thermal needs are part-selection / characterisation, not heating

**Decision:**

- **The −60 °C rating gap** (ADR-054 open item 2; `docs/analysis/array-topology-fault-tolerance.md`
  §9.5: −55 °C diodes / −40 °C converters against a −60 °C case) is closed by **part selection or
  explicit accepted-and-characterised behaviour** (ADR-043 decision 2), **not** by heating. Holding
  the electronics at −40 °C costs **0.160 W = 41 % of the daylight average** and **173× the bank**
  over a 10 h night; at −20 °C it costs **0.360 W = 93 %** of the average. Both rejected.
- **Supercapacitor cold behaviour** is a **characterisation** problem (ADR-036/ADR-047 open item).
  Warming the storage costs ~4× the usable budget (ADR-043) and is rejected. The quantity to watch is
  **ESR rise**, not capacitance loss: the stored energy is unchanged, but the ESR drop under the
  1.2 A TX pulse eats the 2.4 V headroom to the 3.0 V floor (roughly a `k ≈ 10` ESR rise on the
  doubled bank would consume half of it). **Measure it at −60 °C.**
- **Cell and bond thermal cycling** is the cracking mechanism, and its fix is **ADR-052's end-only
  unbonded mount** — a heater is not the mechanism's remedy. Heater cost for the cells is not
  quantified here (`TODO(unverified)`; the repo carries no cell thermal conductance).

### D6 — The bench measurements are the gate (operator-facing)

Recorded as required pre-flight tests (design work only; nothing is run or ordered here):

1. **Cold-soak frequency-offset measurement** of the chosen reference at
   −60 / −40 / −20 / 0 / +20 / +40 °C → `Δf/f` in ppm. This is ADR-042 §D5's deciding number.
   *Pass:* residual inside the chosen BW/SF carrier-offset budget.
2. **1PPS-discipline convergence test** — run the 1PPS→`Δf/f`→synth-trim loop and log residual ppm
   vs time. *Pass:* converges below **< 0.05 ppm** within a **≤ 100 s** window and holds. This also
   supplies the missing `σ_t`.
3. **Cold (−60 °C) capacitance and ESR** of the fitted bank, feeding D5's sensitivity.
4. **Enclosure thermal step test** — apply known `P`, measure equilibrium `ΔT`, compute `G = P/ΔT`.
   This replaces the assumed `G = 10 mW/K` in ADR-042 §D1, ADR-043 and this record.
5. **Module census resolution** — via mechanical drawings / deshield photos: does the plain
   LoRa2021 break out pin 3 (NTC)? Does the SX1280 module expose a TCXO control pin?

## 5. Consequences

### Positive

- The operator's question is answered **directly and numerically**: no heater provision exists,
  none should be added, and the replacement (module TCXOs + GPS 1PPS discipline) costs **zero grams
  and zero watts**.
- The per-radio reference census and the GPS-discipline provision — the two things ADR-042 does not
  carry — are now recorded, so no future reader hunts for a heater that should not exist.
- The heater rejection is now robust **across a range of `G`**, not only at one assumed value.
- The adjacent thermal needs are each given a cost and a non-heating disposition, so a future
  "keep it warm" proposal must argue against those numbers.

### Costs (accepted)

- Firmware owes the 1PPS-discipline loop and the temperature-triggered recalibration ADR-042 §A4
  already requires.
- The bare-LoRa2021 drift gap is closed by **part choice, an NTC, or discipline** — each a task, not
  a heater.
- Cold bank behaviour and the rating gap remain **open measurements**, carried forward.

---

## 6. What would falsify this

- A measured `G` so low that a turning-point heater costs < ~0.02 W continuous **and** the night
  arithmetic fits the bank — contradicted by the `G = 3 mW/K` estimate already failing by 260×.
- A measured carrier drift smaller than the LoRa/FLRC offset tolerance (ADR-042 §D5 `Δf_max ≤ 0`)
  — this would make *all* mitigations optional; the no-heater conclusion still stands.
- A bench measurement showing the bare crystal's cold drift is inside tolerance **and** GPS
  discipline cannot converge below it — would reopen the part choice, not the heater.
- A measured cold ESR rise so large that the bank cannot deliver one TX burst (ADR-036's own
  falsifier) — reopens buffer sizing, not the thermal strategy.

## 8. Correction (2026-10-07, appended by ADR-057) — the tolerance analysis here is LoRa-based; FLRC governs

**Appended, not a rewrite.** Nothing above is changed or deleted; the history stays legible.
This section records only that one half of this record rests on the wrong modulation.

This record's **carrier-offset-tolerance half** is stated in "LoRa/FLRC" terms: §D3's pass
criterion and open items 4 and 6 both use a LoRa/BW-SF budget, and §D1/§D6 name "the
LoRa/FLRC carrier-offset budget" as the gate.

**The vehicle flies FLRC, not LoRa, and FLRC's tolerance is materially different.** FLRC's
tolerance is **band-independent in absolute Hz** — about **±64–76 kHz at 650 kbps** (Semtech
AN1200.101 §5.2 Tables 3/4/5, pp. 8–9, verified against the in-tree PDF) — which is
**≈±148–175 ppm at 433 MHz but only ≈±26–32 ppm at 2.4 GHz**. LoRa's tolerance is
proportional to bandwidth (±25 %BW legacy / ±33 %BW Gen4 extended; ±100 ppm at SF12;
LR2021 datasheet Rev. 2.2 Table 3-17). **The FLRC budget is tighter in ppm at 2.4 GHz than
the LoRa figures this record used, not looser.**

**Consequences for this record:**

- **§D3's pass criterion and §D6's cold-soak pass criterion must use the FLRC budget**, i.e.
  **±26–32 ppm at 2.4 GHz at 650 kbps** — not a LoRa BW/SF figure.
- **Open item 4** ("The LoRa/FLRC carrier-offset tolerance for the chosen BW/SF") is resolved
  to FLRC for the flight link; its LoRa half no longer governs.
- **Open item 6** (the module census) is unaffected by the modulation correction and is
  repeated in ADR-057's open items because the on-chip NTC route turns on it.
- **The no-heater decision (§D1), the per-radio reference census (§D2) and the GPS 1PPS
  discipline provision (§D3's mechanism) stand unchanged**, and §D3's correction mechanism (a
  measured, table-free path) is consistent with ADR-057's Rank-1 mitigation. **No `GetFreqError`
  readout and no AFC were found**, so that mechanism must measure via a 1PPS-gated cycle
  counter, not a radio register — recorded in ADR-057.

**See ADR-057** (`docs/adr/057-flrc-drift-strategy.md`, Status Proposed) for the FLRC
strategy: the band-independence fact, the per-band verdict, the corrected degradation ladder
(never reduce the FLRC rate), the corrected Tx/Rx budget understanding, and the ranked
mitigations.

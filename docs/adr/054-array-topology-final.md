## 1. Context (the evidence, from both analyses)

### 1.1 The deciding number is the ARRAY-SIDE CURRENT at 7.2 W

Both analyses independently reduce the topology question to one number — the current the array must
deliver at its 7.2 W peak (12 cells × 0.5 V × 1.2 A):

| Topology | Array-side current at 7.2 W | I²R vs series | 4.0 × 1.2 mm tab-land margin (2.73 A rating) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12-cell series (incumbent)** | **1.20 A** | **1×** | **2.27× — legal** |
| 2S2P | **2.40 A** | 4× | 1.14× |
| 4P × 3S (wings parallel, 1.5 V) | **4.80 A** | 16× | **0.57× — FAILS** |
| 12P all-parallel (0.5 V) | **14.40 A** | 144× | **0.19× — FAILS** |

The array-side current simultaneously sets (i) the I²R penalty, (ii) whether the existing
4.0 × 1.2 mm wing tab lands stay legal (`docs/analysis/wing-electrical.md` §6.3: 2.73 A at
ΔT = 10 °C, 1 oz), and (iii) whether *any* low-quiescent converter can carry the array at all.
**Only at 1.20 A does a verified converter exist that also meets the night budget, and only at
1.20 A do the existing tab lands stay legal.**

### 1.2 The parallel families are BLOCKED BY AVAILABLE SILICON — not by physics, not by preference

Both analyses searched for a part that could serve a parallel bus and found none:

- **Every sub-2.5 V-input part tops out far short of 7.2 W.** The BQ25570's peak output is
  **110 mA = 0.59 W** (systematic §5.4; adversarial datasheet read, its Rev G `SLUSBH2G`), and it is
  **blocked outright by its 510 mW `PIN_PK` absolute maximum**, which a single 30.6 cm² LARGE cell
  exceeds on its own. The TPS61200 is **600 mA / 3.0 W**; the TPS61099 is **~1.5 W**. Every
  µA-class part (BQ25570 0.1 A, ADP5091/92 0.15 A, LTC3105 0.4 A, TPS61099 ~0.3 A) caps at
  0.1–0.4 A — **4× to 48× short** of the ~1.57 A output the array needs.
- **Every ≥ 12 V-input part starts at 1.8–2.5 V**, so it cannot take a 1.5 V or a 0.5 V bus.
  The verified parts that clear the array's **≈9.58 V cold open-circuit** (ADR-050 §3.2) — the
  only family that survives the series string's cold case — all begin at 1.8–2.5 V.

**So the parallel rearrangements are unavailable for a parts reason.** The adversarial analysis
states it as: *"the parallel topologies are BLOCKED — not merely expensive — by available silicon
at flight mass."*

### 1.3 The one verified part that makes the series family buildable

For the series family exactly one verified part satisfies the input range, the output current and a
night-safe quiescent: the **LTC3115-1** — **2.7–40 V in, 1–2 A out**, with a 3 µA shutdown state
inside ADR-050 §3.6's `I_Q ≤ 5 µA` bound (systematic §5.5). It passes the input range with 4.2×
margin on the 9.58 V cold Voc and passes the output requirement at 6.0 V (2 A step-down =
10.8 W ≥ 7.2 W). It fails the running quiescent (30 µA = 162 µW = 162 % of the 100 µW anchor) —
see Open item (iv).

> **archived copies of vendor product pages** (`web.archive.org`), not from datasheet reads:

## 2. Decision

### 2.1 Decision item 1 — the 12-cell series string is RETAINED

**The array remains 4 wings × 3 LARGE cells = 12 cells in ONE series string** (ADR-006, ADR-049,
ADR-051 §1.1). The deciding number is the **array-side current at 7.2 W: 1.20 A (series) versus
2.40 A (2S2P) / 4.80 A (4P × 3S) / 14.40 A (all-parallel)** (§1.1), and the parallel families are
**blocked by available silicon** (§1.2), not by physics or preference.

**The operator's question — *"avoid series wherever reasonably possible"* — is answered directly,
and the answer is that the lever is wrong for this array:**

1. **The cracked-cell tolerance he wants is obtainable WITHOUT leaving series.** The systematic
   analysis: *"the cracked-cell tolerance the operator wants is available without leaving series"*;
   *"`avoid series wherever reasonably possible` is the wrong lever; `protect the series you have`
   is the right one."* The series family, protected, converts *"a cracked cell kills the string"*
   into **−14.67 % open / −8.33 % short** at cell granularity — a per-cell protection (Decision
   item 3) or, at minimum, the group protection of ADR-053.
2. **The parallel rearrangements are unavailable for a parts reason, not a preference.** No
   sub-2.5 V converter carries 7.2 W with a µA-class quiescent, and no ≥ 12 V one accepts a 1.5 V
   or 0.5 V bus (§1.2). *"Avoiding series"* would therefore require silicon that does not exist in
   a hand-solderable, night-quiet class. This is a **silicon** constraint, and it is the reason the
   answer is what it is.

**Both analyses independently ranked KEEPING the series string first**, the systematic as T6a/T2/T1
and the adversarial as Rank 1 — and both found the parallel families blocked by the same part gap.

### 2.2 Decision item 2 — the HUB-SIDE per-interface bypass diode is MANDATORY and LOAD-BEARING

**One bypass diode per wing, placed on the HUB, across the interface lands `W<n>_SOLAR_P` ↔
`W<n>_SOLAR_N`, rated ≥ 2 A / 40 V** (the existing `D_BP1…D_BP4` provision, ADR-051 §2.4
decision 4.2 / §2.7; ADR-053 §2.7). **Populated, not DNP, on any flight that carries cutters.**

This is the **only** protection a cut cannot jettison, and the **only** element that restores a
broken series **PATH**:

- **A cut removes the wing and every diode soldered to it.** A per-cell or per-wing diode that
  lives on the wing board leaves with the wing; it cannot bridge the gap because it is on the wrong
  side of it (adversarial F3). Only a **hub-side, per-interface** diode can short the removed
  wing's position and keep the string charging.
- **Without it populated, a cutter-carrying flight is WORSE OFF THAN BASELINE: the first cut kills
  the array.** The socket lands go open, the string opens, and the array delivers **0.00 W** — a
  100 % loss. The adversarial analysis states it plainly: *"a flight that carries cutters and has
  per-cell diodes but no hub-side per-interface diodes is strictly worse off than the baseline —
  the first cut kills the array, and the 12 per-cell diodes did nothing."*

**Both analyses rank this mandatory** (systematic §3.1, §4.2, §7; adversarial Rank 2 and F3). It
**strengthens** ADR-051 §2.4 decisions 4.2 and 4.3, and it is the placement correction this record
also applies to ADR-053 (§3.2).

### 2.3 Decision item 3 — PER-CELL bypass diodes are REJECTED

Per-cell bypass (one diode across each of the 12 cells) is **rejected as a requirement**. The two
decisive reasons are the adversarial analysis's F4 and F5:

**(a) It buys about 1.2 W of peak on a 15.85× peak-to-average surplus, while adding +24
hand-soldered joints and an end-only mechanical risk.**
- Per-cell retains **86 %** of the array per event versus per-group's **69 %** — a difference of
  **≈ 1.2 W** (adversarial F4: `6.18 − 4.98 = 1.20 W = 16.7 % of the array`). The mission average
  is **0.388 W** and the radio peak **6.1495 W**, a **15.85×** peak-to-average surplus
  (`docs/analysis/array-power-architecture.md` §3.2). *"Per-cell granularity is buying ≈1.2 W of
  peak power that no requirement in the repo asks for."*
- **+24 hand-soldered joints** (6 per wing × 4; ADR-046/`wing-electrical` §2.3: 8 joints per wing,
  32 per aircraft). That is **+75 %** on a 12-cell array — from 32 joints to 56. The project already
  treats joint count as a first-class cost (ADR-049's "108 hand-soldered joints per aircraft
  instead of 36").
- On the **END-ONLY, no-adhesive-bond** mount (ADR-052) there is no board face behind the cell, so a
  per-cell diode would require a **free-standing 2-lead bridge over a 0.21 mm bare wafer** — exactly
  the unsupported spanning element the spine-and-ribs frame exists to avoid (adversarial F5).

**(b) A SINGLE REVERSED DIODE IS FORWARD-BIASED BY ITS OWN CELL'S 0.5 V AND SILENTLY SHORTS OUT
THAT CELL BY CONSTRUCTION — i.e. it creates the hard-short failure mode it was fitted to prevent.**
A bypass diode for cell *k* is normal-operation **reverse**-biased (anode → the cell's LOW node,
cathode → the cell's HIGH node). Install it the other way round and it is **forward-biased by the
cell's own 0.5 V**: it clamps the cell to ≈ 0.35 V and shunts it, so the cell produces ≈ 0 V while
the diode carries the full 1.2 A continuously (adversarial F5.3). *"Twelve diodes in a hand-built
array are twelve chances to do this; nothing in the topology catches it (it is invisible to a
string-voltage check, because the loss is only 0.85 V of 6.0 V)."*

**THE ONE CONDITION THAT REOPENS THIS.** Per-cell bypass becomes **load-bearing for hot-spot
safety** — and must be revisited — **if the operator's bench crack test shows SOFT SHUNTS dominate**
(adversarial §5.1; Open item (i)). On a soft shunt, only a per-cell diode clamps the failing cell:
a per-wing diode cannot see it, because the wing still sources positive voltage (adversarial F2).
If soft shunts dominate, the per-cell granularity moves from Rank 3 to Rank 2 and the F5 cost
becomes a cost worth paying. **Until that is measured, the rejection stands.**

> This rejection is consistent with **ADR-053's retained conclusion** (per-group over per-cell),
> which this record **confirms and strengthens** (§3.2). It is also the condition under which
> ADR-053's grouping recommendation reverses — ADR-053 §5 already names it.

### 2.4 Decision item 4 — THE MECHANISM CORRECTION (the earlier characterisation was wrong)

**A bypass diode does NOT "make the converter tolerate" cell failures. Two different requirements
were credited to the wrong part. This is corrected explicitly.**

The mechanism, from the adversarial analysis F1/F7/F9:

- **The converter already tolerates the VOLTAGE consequence of every cell failure, including a hard
  short.** Its wide input window (2.5–12 V for the verified TPS63060 class) absorbs the loss of a
  cell's 0.5 V with no diode at all.
- **For an OPEN cell, the diode RESTORES THE PATH.** Without it the string is broken; with it the
  string continues at one cell/group less voltage. This is a property of the **wiring**, not of the
  converter.
- **For a HARD SHORT (R ≈ 0), the diode never reaches its forward threshold and never conducts —
  and it is NOT NEEDED.** The short itself carries the string current (`I·R ≈ 0 < V_f`), the string
  simply loses that cell's 0.5 V, and the converter's 2.5–12 V window absorbs it.
- **For a SOFT SHUNT, the diode acts as a HOT-SPOT CLAMP** — it turns on only when
  `1.2 A × R_shunt > V_f`, clamping the cell and shunting the remainder. The adversarial worked
  number: a 1 Ω shunt dissipates `1.2² × 1 = 1.44 W = 47.1 mW/cm²` (2.4× nameplate) without the
  diode, versus `0.35² / 1 = 0.12 W` with the per-cell clamp — a 12× reduction (adversarial F1.3).

**Stated plainly:** a bypass diode is a **path-restoration device** (open) and a **hot-spot clamp**
(soft shunt), and it is **inert** on a hard short. It is *not* a converter-tolerance device. The
converter needs the shunt clamp (ADR-049 clause 3 / ADR-047 §7 / ADR-050 §3.5), not the diodes;
the diodes need the converter's window, not the other way round. **This corrects the earlier
manager statement that one diode covers "open, shorted and shaded" as if by one mechanism** — the
three modes are handled by three different mechanisms, and on the hard-short mode by no diode at
all.

### 2.5 Decision item 5 — the night-survival scare was OVERSTATED; the sizing criterion is the WINTER DAY-MEAN, not the night

**The winter night is NOT a binding constraint, and it must not be restated as one.** The
arithmetic (systematic §6):

```
day length (50.0 °N, winter solstice)   =  7.852 h       (wing-omnidirectional.md; H0 = 58.8884°)
dark                                    = 16.148 h      (≈16.15 h)
night anchor (ADR-036)                  = 100 µW
night energy  = 100 µW × 16.148 h × 3600 s = 5.81 J
usable bank (ADR-047 §3.2)              = 33.264 J      -> covers the night 5.7× over
daytime load  = 0.388 W × 7.852 h       = 10 968 J
required day-mean array power = (10 968 + 5.81) J / (7.852 × 3600) s = 388.206 mW
                                vs 388.000 mW for the load alone
                                -> +205.7 µW = +0.05 %
```

**The night adds only ≈ +205.7 µW = +0.05 % to the 0.388 W requirement.** The reason is that the
night load is the **100 µW anchor**, not the 0.388 W mission average, and the 3.3 F bank holds
**5.7×** the night's energy. **The correct sizing criterion is therefore the WINTER DAY-MEAN
INSOLATION over the 7.852 h day**, i.e. `0.388 W / (density × rotation factor)` (64.9–106.1 cm²
for the hub array, ADR-051 §1.4/§1.6) — **not** the night. This record does **not** restate the
night as a binding constraint.

## 3. Corrections to records already on `main`

Both corrections below are **explicit**, **cited to the two analyses**, **appended** to the records
they fix (their bodies and original numbers are preserved so the history stays legible), and each
carries a one-line pointer in the fixed record's header.

### 3.1 ADR-051's cut ladder is numerically WRONG — and it is safety-relevant

**What ADR-051 records (ADR-051 §1.1 and its §2.4 table):** the ladder **6.0 / 4.5 / 3.0 / 1.5 V**
for 0–3 cuts, with the 2-cut row (3.0 V) marked *"yes"* (charging at ADR-050 §3.2's 2.5 V floor).

**Why it is wrong.** ADR-051's **own decision 4.2** mandates a **hub bypass diode that SITS IN THE
STRING**: after a cut, the current flows *through* that interface's bypass diode, adding its
forward drop to the string (ADR-051 §2.4 decision 4.2/4.3). **The drops ADD ACROSS CUTS.** The
ladder must subtract one `V_f` per cut:

```
0 cuts : 12 cells            = 6.00 V   (ADR-051 table: 6.00)  charges
1 cut  :  9 cells − 1×0.38 V = 4.12 V   (ADR-051 table: 4.50)  charges
2 cuts :  6 cells − 2×0.38 V = 2.24 V   (ADR-051 table: 3.00)  DOES NOT CHARGE   ← the wrong row
3 cuts :  3 cells − 3×0.38 V = 0.36 V   (ADR-051 table: 1.50)  does not charge
4 cuts :  0 cells            = 0.00 V   (ADR-051 table: 0.00)  no charge
```

**The corrected ladder is 6.00 / 4.12 / 2.24 / 0.36 V.** (The 0.38 V figure is the bypass diode's
drop at 1.2 A interpolated from the PMEG4030ER datasheet's 460 mV at 3 A — the adversarial
analysis uses 0.35 V and reaches the same conclusion at 4.15 / 2.30 / 0.45 V. The conversion to
"does it charge" is **robust to the choice**: 2 cuts charges only if `V_f ≤ 0.25 V`, and by the
LTC3115-1's 2.7 V floor only if `V_f ≤ 0.15 V` — neither is met by any ≥ 2 A / 40 V Schottky.)

**Consequence — stated plainly because it bears on the operator's requirement *"I want to shed to
survive"*.** The **2-cut case at 2.24 V is BELOW THE FLOOR OF EVERY VERIFIED WIDE-INPUT PART**
(2.5 V for the TPS63060 class; 2.7 V for the LTC3115-1, the only part that also meets the night
budget). **So the vehicle survives ONE cut — plus whatever the independent hub array can carry.**
That is a materially shallower ladder than ADR-051 §2.4 records, and it is a fact about the very
bypass diode ADR-051's own decision 4.2 mandates. **This makes the hub array stop being optional at
TWO cuts, not three** (adversarial F6).

**A record already disagrees with it:** `docs/analysis/array-power-architecture.md` §3.2 uses a
**0.3 V** diode drop and therefore gives **4.20 V** (3 wings) and **2.70 V** (2 wings) — i.e. it
**includes** the drop ADR-051's table omits, and its 2-wing figure is likewise below the wide-input
floor. The two records disagree and **ADR-051's table is the one without the drop**; this correction
cross-references that document.

### 3.2 ADR-053's diode placement is wrong for the cut case

**What ADR-053 records:** its §1.7 (titled *"Placement consequence — the diodes live on the WING
boards"*) and §2.5 (titled *"the diodes live on the WING boards (and on the hub array)"*) place the
diodes on the **WING boards**, offering the hub as an *alternative* for the per-group case
(§1.7: the per-group diode *"can sit either on the hub … or on the wing board itself"*).

**Why that is wrong.** **If the per-group diodes are on the wing boards, a cut jettisons them and
the array dies on the first cut** (adversarial F3). The wing is jettisonable; a diode soldered to
the wing leaves with it and cannot bridge the gap. **The placement must be HUB-SIDE PER-INTERFACE**
(for any cutter-carrying flight) — across `W<n>_SOLAR_P` ↔ `W<n>_SOLAR_N`, which is where the
`D_BP1…D_BP4` provision already IS (ADR-051 §2.4 decision 4.2). The wing-board option must not be
used for a cutter-carrying flight, and ADR-053's §1.7/§2.5 wing-board emphasis is corrected by this
placement amendment.

**ADR-053's RETAINED conclusion — per-GROUP over per-CELL — is CONFIRMED and STRENGTHENED by the
adversarial analysis.** ADR-053 §1.6/§2.4 recommends per-group of 3 (4 diodes) over per-cell
(12 diodes) on a **512 mg** mass delta and a granularity improvement the 0.388 W budget does not
need; the adversarial analysis reaches the same ranking independently (§2.3/**F4**: per-cell buys
≈1.2 W of peak on a 15.85× surplus; **F5**: the buildability and reversed-diode risks). The
per-group conclusion is therefore **strengthened, not weakened** — with the one reopening
condition of Decision item 3 (soft shunts dominating).

## 4. Open items / measurements (carried forward, none invented)

1. **THE HIGHEST-VALUE MEASUREMENT both analyses independently demanded — is a −60 °C thermal
   crack on this cell class an OPEN or a SHORT?** The series family is **safe against a short** and
   **exposed to an open**; the parallel families the reverse. The answer changes what needs
   protecting (path restoration vs hot-spot clamping), **and nobody has measured it**
   (systematic §9.6: *"the highest-value measurement in this document"*; adversarial §5.1/§7.2;
   ADR-053 §4.5). If **soft shunts dominate**, per-cell bypass moves from rejected to
   **load-bearing for hot-spot safety** (Decision item 3).
2. **A real rating gap: every verified semiconductor is rated −55 °C (diodes) or −40 °C
   (converters) against a −60 °C design case.** The PMEG4020ER/PMEG4030ER `T_amb` minimum is
   −55 °C; every converter in the survey is −40 °C or −40…85 °C. Whether junction self-heating
   rescues the part, and whether an extended/automotive grade exists, is `TODO(unverified)`
   (systematic §5.3.2, §9.5).
3. **The diode's ≥ 2 A / 3 A ratings are δ = 0.5, 20 kHz ratings, but this bypass path is
   PERMANENT-CONTINUOUS (δ = 1).** Both PMEG datasheets specify `I_F(AV)` at **δ = 0.5, 20 kHz**;
   the δ = 1 (DC) curve (Fig. 9 / Fig. 11) sits below it and falls faster with ambient. **The
   honest check is "1.2 A vs the δ = 1 curve at the flight's ambient", not "1.2 A vs 3 A" — the
   rating may need derating** (systematic §5.3.1, §9.4; ADR-053 §2.2 already extends the duty rule
   to permanent-continuous).
4. **The LTC3115-1 datasheet was never read — its 30 µA running quiescent = 162 µW = 162 % of the
   night anchor, so ADR-050's `I_Q ≤ 5 µA` bound may need to become a HARD FIRMWARE REQUIREMENT
   (shutdown-only).** The 3 µA shutdown / 30 µA running figures are from the **archived ADI product
   page**, not the datasheet (§1.3 caveat). **This is flagged as a DECISION, not decided here:**
   firmware must disable the converter at night (hold it in shutdown) or the 100 µW anchor is
   overdrawn by 62 %. Whether firmware can hold it in shutdown without a keeper, and the
   disabled-state leakage, are both `TODO(unverified)` (systematic §5.5, §9.2).
5. **Whether the hub array (ADR-051 §2.2) also gets per-cell bypass.** Its cells are hub-mounted
   and need their own diodes; per-cell or per-group there is a fresh trade, and it is not decided
   here (ADR-053 §4.3).

## 5. Consequences

### Positive
- **The topology fork is closed and the record is legible.** The operator's question is answered
  with the deciding number (1.20 A) and the parts reason, not with preference.
- **The one load-bearing protection — the hub-side per-interface bypass — is recorded as mandatory
  and is the placement the cut actually needs** (Decision item 2; ADR-053 §3.2 correction).
- **The mechanism is now correct**, so no future reader will look for converter tolerance in a
  diode (Decision item 4).
- **Two numerically-wrong / under-placed records are corrected in place**, with their original
  numbers preserved and a header pointer added (§3).

### Costs (accepted)
- **The cut ladder is shallower than ADR-051 records: ONE cut**, plus whatever the hub array can
  carry (§3.1) — which raises the hub array from a nice-to-have to the thing that makes cuts 2+
  meaningful.
- **The series string carries the 9.58 V cold Voc**, which is why the input rating is ≥ 12 V and
  why the input part set is narrow (§1.2, §1.3).
- **The `I_Q` bound may become a firmware mandate, not a part-selection preference** (Open item 4).

## 7. Notes

- **Why one ADR and not three.** The topology decision, the ADR-051 ladder correction and the
  ADR-053 placement correction are one logical change to one decision — the array topology — and
  they were produced together from the same two analyses. The two corrections are **appended** to
  their records (not rewritten), so the history stays legible and no original number is deleted.
- **What is NOT superseded.** ADR-051 §1.1's core claim (a cut removes string voltage; the hub
  array is the enabling condition), ADR-051 §2.4 decision 4.2 (populate the per-interface diodes)
  and decision 4.3 (the bypass path is continuous), and ADR-051 §2.1's independence invariant are
  all **retained**; only the ladder *table* is corrected. ADR-053's per-group-over-per-cell
  conclusion is **confirmed and strengthened**; only its *placement* is corrected.
- **The two analyses did not read each other** (the adversarial one was explicitly not told the
  systematic one's conclusion), and they converged on the decision by different routes — which is
  why this record treats the fork as settled rather than as one document's opinion.
- **No hardware is ordered by this record.** It is design work only.

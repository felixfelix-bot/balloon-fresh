## 1. Context — two existing records rest on a good answer to the wrong question

`docs/adr/042-thermal-drift-strategy.md` and `docs/adr/056-thermal-and-frequency-drift.md`
both size the drift problem against a **LoRa** carrier-offset budget. ADR-042 §D5 makes
"LoRa carrier-offset tolerance for the chosen BW/SF" the gate, and ADR-056 §D2/§D3 and its
open item 4 use "the LoRa/FLRC carrier-offset budget" as the pass criterion. The operator
has since stated that the vehicle flies **FLRC, not LoRa**. FLRC's frequency-offset
tolerance is materially different from LoRa's, and — crucially — **not** in the direction

## 2. Decision

### D1 — The governing fact: FLRC's tolerance is band-independent in absolute Hz

AN1200.101 §5.2 states the mechanism in one sentence, quoted verbatim from the in-tree PDF
(`docs/lr2021-research/semtech-official/AN1200.101_LR2021_FLRC_Improvements.pdf`, Rev. 1.0,
June 2026, p. 8):

> "The following table shows the permissible offset as a function of both data rate and
> payload length for 10% packet error rate. … this effect can be avoided by keeping the
> **total frequency error between Tx and Rx** less than the frequency limits below."

**Table 3** ("Measured Typical Frequency Error Tolerance [kHz] of the FLRC Modem versus
Data Rate for **all Frequency Bands**") gives the tolerance in **absolute kHz**, independent
of band — verified at pp. 8–9. The load-bearing row is **650 kbps**:

| Payload | 650 kbps | 520 kbps | 325 kbps | 260 kbps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 B | ±76 kHz | ±59 | ±37 | ±30 |
| 31 B | ±73 | ±57 | ±36 | ±29 |
| 100 B | ±69 | ±53 | ±34 | ±27 |
| 200 B | ±67 | ±51 | ±32 | ±26 |
| 300 B | ±66 | ±50 | ±32 | ±25 |
| 400 B | ±65 | ±50 | ±32 | ±25 |
| 511 B | ±64 | ±50 | ±31 | ±25 |

So **FLRC at 650 kbps tolerates about ±64–76 kHz** (511 B … 10 B), the same **absolute** Hz
budget in every band. The same tables expressed in **ppm** are given separately per band
(AN1200.101 Tables 4 and 5, verified pp. 8–9), and that is the whole story:

| FLRC rate | tolerance (Hz) | at 433.05 MHz | at 2.4 GHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2600 kbps | ±206 kHz | ±476 ppm | ±86 ppm |
| 1300 kbps | ±119 kHz | ±275 ppm | ±50 ppm |
| 1040 kbps | ±102 kHz | ±236 ppm | ±42 ppm |
| **650 kbps** | **±64 kHz** (511 B) | **±148 ppm** | **±27 ppm** |
| 520 kbps | ±50 kHz | ±115 ppm | ±21 ppm |
| 325 kbps | ±31 kHz | ±72 ppm | ±13 ppm |
| 260 kbps | ±25 kHz | ±58 ppm | ±10 ppm |

(Reference frequencies 433.05 MHz — the low edge of our licence-exempt band, ADR-041 — and
2.4 GHz; the AN1200.101 table heading is "2.45 GHz". Arithmetic: 64 kHz / 433.05 MHz =
147.8 ppm; 64 kHz / 2400 MHz = 26.7 ppm; 76 kHz / 433.05 MHz = 175.5 ppm; 76 kHz / 2400 MHz
= 31.7 ppm. The ppm budget is therefore **≈5.5× tighter at 2.4 GHz** than at 433 MHz — the
ratio of the carriers, 2400/433.05 = 5.54; ≈5.7× against the table's 2.45 GHz heading,
2450/433.05 = 5.66.)

The vendor datasheet corroborates the absolute-Hz, band-independent reading. LR2021
datasheet Rev. 2.2 Table 3-13 (`FRF = 2.4 GHz`, `CR = 3/4`) gives "Max freq error
tolerated" as `FLRC_2600_CR05_FERR` ±150 kHz, `FLRC_1040_CR05_FERR` ±100 kHz,
**`FLRC_650_CR05_FERR` ±70 kHz**, `FLRC_520_CR05_FERR` ±50 kHz, `FLRC_260_CR05_FERR`
±25 kHz (same order as AN1200.101's measured-typical ±64–76 kHz at 650 kbps — a spec floor
vs a measured typical, not a contradiction).

**LoRa for contrast** (LR2021 datasheet Rev. 2.2 Table 3-17 / Table 3-18, verified):
`LORA_FERR_L1` = **±25 %BW** (legacy) and `LORA_FERR_L2` = **±33 %BW** for at most 3 dB
degradation (Gen4 extended); `LORA_FERR_L4` = **±100 ppm at SF12**. LoRa's tolerance is
**proportional to bandwidth**, so it is *not* band-independent in Hz (a fixed BW is a
different ppm at each band) — the asymmetry between the two modulations is the point.

**This is the design-critical fact:** the drift budget is an **absolute-Hz** budget whose
*ppm equivalent* shrinks as the carrier rises. Low band is the drift-friendly band.

### D2 — Per-band verdict

| Link | Balloon role | Reference | FLRC 650 kbps budget | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **433.05–434.79 MHz downlink** | **TX** (`LoRa2021F33-2G4`, built-in 0.5 ppm TCXO) | 0.5 ppm at 433.05 MHz = **±0.217 kHz** | ±64–76 kHz | **~50–60× margin. No mitigation needed.** |
| **2.4 GHz uplink** | **RX** (bare `LoRa2021`, crystal only) | ±10 ppm spec (NiceRF V1.3) to ±20–30 ppm board-level | ±64–76 kHz = **±26–32 ppm** | **OUTSIDE the budget** with a second crystal; only **~1.5× inside** against a TCXO-class ground reference; inside only at ≥ ~2080 kbps |

- **433 MHz downlink — no mitigation.** The balloon transmits from the F33's 0.5 ppm TCXO:
  ±0.217 kHz at 433.05 MHz against the ±64 kHz budget is ≈290×; the analysis's more
  conservative summary table (`docs/analysis/meshcore-lr2021-drift.md` §6.3) states
  **~50–60× margin** using a deliberately worst-case ±1.2 kHz reference error, and §R7
  states ~100× for the balloon-TX-alone case. Whichever bound is taken, the 433 MHz
  downlink is **immune to the drift question** — tens to hundreds of times inside. Nothing
  is required here.
- **2.4 GHz uplink — this is the exposure.** A crystal-referenced receiver against a
  crystal-referenced ground transmitter is ~±40–60 ppm combined (±96–144 kHz at 2.4 GHz)
  against a ±64 kHz budget: **outside**. Even against a perfect TCXO ground transmitter
  (~20.5 ppm combined for a ±20 ppm crystal) it is only **~1.5× inside** at 650 kbps and
  **outside at 325 and 260 kbps**. **The drift problem is specifically the 2.4 GHz uplink
  with a bare crystal.**

### D3 — CORRECTED requirement: the degradation ladder must NOT reduce the FLRC rate

**This corrects any degradation ladder — including the firmware duty ladder proposed
earlier — that contains an FLRC rate-reduction rung.** Lowering the FLRC rate makes the
offset budget **worse in ppm terms**, because tolerance scales *with* rate (D1's table):
650 → 520 → 325 → 260 kbps takes the 2.4 GHz budget from **±27 ppm → ±21 → ±13 → ±10 ppm**.
A slower rung is *more* likely to fail on drift, not less. This is the opposite of the LoRa
instinct, where a *wider* bandwidth buys tolerance and a slower link is the safe fallback.
**For FLRC the drift-robust setting is the fast one.**

**Recorded requirement (normative):**

1. **No FLRC rate-reduction rung.** A ladder step that lowers the FLRC bit rate is
   self-defeating and must not be used as a drift response.
2. The correct degrade paths, if the 2.4 GHz offset budget is what is at risk, are:
   - **switch reference** to a TCXO-bearing path (D5(ii)/(iii)); or
   - **fall back to 433 MHz**, where FLRC's ppm budget is ≈5.5× looser and the balloon
     transmits with the F33's 0.5 ppm TCXO (D2); or
   - **accept a shorter 2.4 GHz window** — fewer, longer attempts — rather than a slower
     modulation.
3. If a rate change is ever made, it must go **up** (toward 2600 kbps, ±86 ppm at 2.4 GHz),
   not down, and it trades an offset budget for a dB budget — decide it with the ADR-041
   link-budget tool, not by intuition.

This **constrains any existing or future degrade-ladder document, including the manager's
earlier firmware duty ladder**; that ladder is hereby recorded as carrying a corrected
requirement, not as an accepted one. (No committed record currently states a
rate-reduction rung — ADR-055 §D3 makes **duty**, not modulation rate, the array-size knob —
so this requirement is a constraint on the ladder going forward.)

### D4 — CORRECTED understanding: a bare-crystal RECEIVER is as exposed as a bare-crystal TRANSMITTER

The AN1200.101 quote in D1 says it directly: the tables bound the "**total frequency error
between Tx and Rx**". The budget is the **maximum error between the two references**, so a
crystal receiver consumes the same budget as a crystal transmitter. The TX-side error shifts
the carrier the receiver must acquire; the RX-side error shifts the receiver's own
acquisition centre. They add.

**This corrects a claim of our own.** Our project's comment in the upstream MeshCore
"NiceRF LoRa2021 (LR2021 Gen 4) variant support" thread asserted:

> "the base module crystal at stratosphere temps is one more reason our balloon payload
> stays RX-only (**TX drifts, RX tolerates**)"

(recorded, with its external provenance, in `docs/analysis/meshcore-lr2021-drift.md` §2.3 and
corrected in §6.2 R8). The **TX/RX asymmetry is real**, but "RX tolerates" is **false in the
absolute**: the receiver's own reference error is not tolerated away — it is half of the
budget. The sentence reads as a reason to stop worrying about the 2.4 GHz RX, when the
2.4 GHz RX (a bare crystal against a crystal ground transmitter, D2) is precisely where the
budget is tightest. **Recorded as corrected.**

### D5 — The ranked mitigations for the 2.4 GHz uplink

Each option is ranked by the analysis's own ordering (a guarantee beats a calibration; zero
mass beats added mass), with the reason it is or is not *available* to us today. Rank 1 is
the preferred disposition; the enumerator letters (i)–(v) are kept for traceability to the
analysis.

**Rank 1 — (iv) GPS 1PPS discipline via the 1PPS-gated cycle counter. Available now, zero
grams, zero watts.** The MAX-M10S is on the board with its 1PPS routed (`GNSS_PPS` to
GPIO21, `docs/adr/108-f33-sx1280-pin-plan.md`), and four accepted ADRs already make GPS UTC
the timing authority. **Constraint recorded by the analysis:** no `GetFreqError`-style
carrier-offset readout and **no AFC** were found in the LR2021 datasheet Rev. 2.2 or in the
AN1200.101/102 texts (`TODO(unverified)` in the analysis — absence in the documents grepped
is not proof of absence in silicon). The closed loop must therefore **measure via a
1PPS-gated cycle counter** counting the radio reference between 1PPS edges — **not** by
reading a radio register. This is the extension to ADR-042 §D3 that ADR-056 §D3 already
records; this ADR adds only the FLRC-correction that its pass criterion is the FLRC budget
(±26–32 ppm at 650 kbps), not a LoRa one.

**Rank 2 — (i) the LR2021's own on-chip temperature compensation (`SetTempCompCfg` 0x0132 +
`SetNtcParams` 0x0133). Best hardware fix if it can be enabled; blocked on a fact we have
not verified.** Verified in LR2021 datasheet Rev. 2.2 §6.12.1/§6.12.2 (Tables 6-69/6-70):

- `SetTempCompCfg` (0x0132) "configures the heating compensation block in Tx if an XTAL
  32MHz is used", with `ntc` (1 = enable NTC) and `comp_mode` (Disabled / Relative /
  Absolute); `SetNtcParams` (0x0133) enters `ntc_r_ratio` and `ntc_beta`.
- **It is available ONLY on a crystal-configured module: "If a TCXO was configured this
  command fails."** So it is **mutually exclusive** with the TCXO route (ii)/(iii) — an
  either/or, not a complement.
- **It needs a POPULATED NTC thermistor next to the crystal.** The bare NiceRF LoRa2021
  module "does not place the NTC" (community teardown reported in the analysis §2.3), and
  ADR-042 Addendum A2 records the same for our four owned plain modules (crystal, no TCXO,
  no NTC).
- **Its own stated purpose is TX self-heating, not ambient stratospheric cold** ("to limit
  frequency drift during high power transmissions"), and the compensation only *helps* — it
  gives no guaranteed ppm figure outside the crystal's characterised −20…+70 °C window.

  **The single blocker:** whether the NTC input pin is broken out on the owned bare NiceRF
  module's castellations is **UNVERIFIED** (open item 1). If it is not, this option is
  unavailable regardless of driver work.

**Rank 3 — (ii) an external TCXO on the SX1280's XTA pin 4, (iii) or a module/board that
breaks out XTA. A guarantee rather than a calibration, but it needs a board/architecture
change.** The two options are the same physics on different carriers and are ranked
together.

- **The SX1280 CAN accept an external clock.** Semtech `DS.SX1280-1.W.APP` Rev 1.0
  (February 2017, not vendored in-tree; read in `docs/analysis/external-clock-and-ocxo.md`
  §A3): pin 4 = `XTA`, "Reference oscillator connection or TCXO input", AC-coupled into XTA
  per §14.2 / Figure 14-3. **Correction to the earlier premise: there is NO on-chip DIO3
  TCXO-enable on the SX1280.** In Figure 14-3 the TCXO has its own rail and DIO3 is merely
  **pulled to VDD_RADIO through a 0 Ω resistor (R15)** — an ordinary multi-purpose I/O, not
  a TCXO power switch. **Gating the TCXO therefore needs an EXTERNAL LOAD SWITCH** (or MCU
  GPIO) on the TCXO's own rail; the SX1280 pin plan (ADR-108) already brings out spare
  control I/O (`SX1280_DIO2`/`DIO3`/`ANT_SW`).
- **But the balloon's 2.4 GHz uplink RX is the bare `LoRa2021`, not the SX1280** (ADR-034
  D4). That module **cannot accept an external clock at all**: its pad 13 is `VTCXO`, an
  **output** ("Can provide power for an external TCXO", NiceRF `LoRa2021` V1.3 §7), and
  **XTA is not broken out on its 18 castellations** (verified §7 pad list: 1 VCC ·
  2,8,11,12,18 GND · 3 MISO · 4 MOSI · 5 SCK · 6 NSS · 7 BUSY · 9 ANT · 10 2.4/S_ANTA ·
  13 VTCXO · 14 RST · 15 DIO9 · 16 DIO8 · 17 DIO7). So making the TCXO route real means
  either **moving the 2.4 GHz RX to the SX1280** (ADR-034 D6 records this as the
  deliberately-not-silently-chosen alternative — 3 RF parts), or **selecting a different
  module / a bare-chip carrier that breaks out XTA**.
- **Or the F33, which is already TCXO-referenced.** The F33 carries a built-in 0.5 ppm TCXO
  (Rev 1.1 cover + §6 `Frequency Error = 0.5 ppm`) that is **non-overridable and always-on`,
  and the module exposes no clock pin (all 18 pins, §7 verified). It cannot be *fed* a
  clock, but it *is* the TCXO-bearing radio we already have; the analysis's R2 suggests
  covering 2.4 GHz RX with it if the architecture permits.
- **Caveat that has not been resolved:** whether the F33's 2.4 GHz RX path can be used
  simultaneously with its 433 MHz TX role (one half-duplex part, two bands, TDM) is not
  resolved (`TODO(unverified)`). Also, **the SX1280's own FLRC tolerance is not established
  by any source found** (open item 2).

**Rank 4 (rejected) — (v) a heater / OCXO. NOT an option; arithmetically and structurally
impossible.** ADR-042 §D1 already rejects a board heater; ADR-056 §D1 re-derives the
rejection as `P = G·ΔT`. An oscillator *oven* fails the same physics at smaller scale, on
the numbers in `docs/analysis/external-clock-and-ocxo.md` Part B:

- Cold-scaled steady-state power is **0.34 W (SiTime SiT5501) to 5.2 W (Raltron OXD30)** —
  the study's two documented parts scaled to the −55 °C ambient. (Both cold figures are
  **arithmetic estimates**, not datasheet tables — neither OCXO datasheet prints a
  below-25 °C current table; marked `TODO(unverified)` there.)
- Against the **33.264 J usable bank**, that is **6–98 s** of bank life (5.2 W → ≈6 s;
  0.34 W → ≈98 s).
- Against one **10-hour night** it is **≈370× (0.34 W) to ≈5 600× (5.2 W)** short.
- **And the structural killer: an oven cannot be switched off.** It holds a crystal at a
  set point *continuously* to be stable, so it pays that power **at night, when there is no
  sun** — while a TCXO can be duty-cycled to **0 µW at night** because the radio is
  daylight-only (ADR-036). A duty-cycled oven is a contradiction in terms. (On the SiT5501,
  disabling the output drops current 44 mA → 43 mA: the oven *is* the load.)

The heater/OCXO option is therefore **excluded on the energy store alone**, independent of
whether FLRC drift needs a fix.

### D6 — What the operator must choose between, and the one measurement that decides it

**The choice the operator faces is between two *kinds* of fix, once the measurement says a
fix is needed:**

- **A firmware discipline fix (Rank 1, GPS 1PPS)** — zero mass, zero added power, reuses
  the MAX-M10S already on the board; cost is firmware and a measured convergence figure.
- **A hardware reference fix (Rank 2 on-chip NTC, or Rank 3 TCXO on XTA)** — a guaranteed
  compensated reference, but Rank 2 is gated on the NTC pin being broken out (UNVERIFIED)
  and Rank 3 is gated on an architecture/board change (2.4 GHz RX moved to the SX1280, or a
  module that breaks out XTA) plus an external load switch.

Rank 2 and Rank 3 are **mutually exclusive** (the on-chip NTC command fails if a TCXO is
configured, D5(ii)); Rank 1 composes with either.

**The ONE measurement that decides it — the deciding number — is the cold-soak
carrier-drift sweep of the actual 2.4 GHz bare-crystal reference**, giving the board-level
temperature coefficient:

> `Δf_max = (measured carrier drift from −60 °C to +40 °C) − (FLRC carrier-offset budget
> for the chosen rate)` — the FLRC budget being **±26–32 ppm** at 650 kbps (D1).

This is the same deciding number ADR-042 §D5 names, but with the **FLRC** budget substituted
for the LoRa one. Procedure (analysis §5.5, matching ADR-042 §D3/§D5): soak the module at
−60 / −40 / −20 / 0 / +20 / +40 °C with ≥ 20 min per step; measure the carrier with an SDR
or counter; fit `Δf(T)`; the **slope** is the drift figure and the **intercept** is the fixed
offset a CP trim would remove; then run a 2.4 GHz FLRC 650 kbps link at both temperature
extremes and count PER against the budget.

- If `Δf_max ≤ 0`, **no mitigation is needed** (and the no-heater conclusion stands). This
  is unlikely for two crystals (D2) but must be measured, not assumed.
- If `Δf_max > 0`, its **magnitude** decides between the Rank-1 firmware fix and a
  Rank-2/Rank-3 hardware reference fix.

**Two facts that gate the choice but are not bench measurements** (they belong in Open
items): whether the NTC input pin is broken out (Rank 2 on/off), and whether the SX1280's
FLRC tolerance is within budget (Rank 3 viability). The NTC pin is **the single blocker for
the on-chip route**.

## 3. Open items

1. **Is the NTC input pin broken out on the owned bare NiceRF `LoRa2021` module?**
   **UNVERIFIED — the single blocker for the Rank-2 on-chip route.** Settled by the module
   mechanical drawing (the module's pin-out, not the chip's) or a **deshield photo** of a
   plain sample. ADR-042 Addendum A2 already carries this open item; it is repeated here
   because D5(ii) turns on it.
2. **The SX1280's own FLRC frequency-offset tolerance — `TODO(unverified)`.** **Every FLRC
   figure in this ADR is LR2021/LR20xx-only** (AN1200.101 and LR2021 datasheet Rev. 2.2).
   No equivalent frequency-error-tolerance table for the SX1280 was found, and none is
   assumed. **Do not apply D1's numbers to the SX1280 without its own source** — this gates
   Rank 3(ii) if the 2.4 GHz RX is re-pointed at the SX1280.
3. **The unmeasured board-level ppm/°C of our actual 2.4 GHz carrier board.** Unknown and
   unmeasured; the MeshCore thread never reported its fridge/freezer result, and the ±10 ppm
   figure is a *crystal* spec, not a *board* figure. This is the drift half of D6's deciding
   number and the reason the measurement is required rather than assumed.
4. **Whether the F33's 2.4 GHz RX path can be used simultaneously with its 433 MHz TX role**
   (one half-duplex part, two bands, TDM) — unresolved; gates the "cover 2.4 GHz RX with the
   F33" variant of Rank 3.
5. **SX1280/TCXO details if Rank 3 is pursued** (carried from the external-clock analysis,
   unchanged): the Figure 14-3 tip values, the "32.0 MHz" vs `FXOSC` 52 MHz label
   discrepancy, and the TCXO drive level into XTA are all `TODO(unverified)`.
6. **Whether the on-chip `SetTempCompCfg` partially compensates ambient cold** (as opposed
   to TX self-heating) is not established by any document read — the datasheet's own framing
   is self-heating. The swap path is `SetXoscCpTrim` (0x0131), a **static** foot-capacitance
   trim with **no temperature term**; it corrects a *fixed* offset only, and the community
   found one trim value did not fit all boards (8,8 vs 3,3 on the same module), so it is a
   per-board calibration to be done at integration, not a firmware constant. Recorded here
   as a fallback, not the plan.

## 5. Consequences

### Positive

- The two existing drift records now rest on the correct modulation. The conclusion that
  changes — FLRC's tolerance is **band-independent in Hz**, so the same ppm budget is
  ≈5.5× tighter at 2.4 GHz than at 433 MHz — is recorded once, with the arithmetic and the
  source.
- The **self-defeating rate-reduction rung** is removed from the degradation ladder before it
  reaches firmware, and the wrong "RX tolerates" framing is corrected in our records.
- The ranked mitigations each carry the reason they are or are not available, so a future
  session does not re-derive the NTC-pin blocker or the SX1280-tolerance gap.

### Costs (accepted)

- Firmware owes the 1PPS-gated cycle-counter measurement (no `GetFreqError`, no AFC) and the
  corrected ladder logic.
- The 2.4 GHz uplink's drift gap is closed only by **discipline, an NTC, a TCXO, or an
  architecture change** — each a task, not a firmware constant.
- The SX1280's FLRC tolerance and the board-level ppm/°C remain **open measurements**.

---

## 6. What would falsify this

- A measured cold-soak drift for the 2.4 GHz bare-crystal reference **inside ±26–32 ppm**
  (D6's `Δf_max ≤ 0`) would make all 2.4 GHz mitigations optional — the 433 MHz verdict and
  the no-heater rejection would still stand.
- A source establishing the **SX1280's** FLRC tolerance at 650 kbps materially *higher* than
  the LR2021's would change Rank 3(ii)'s viability.
- A bench result showing the **F33's 2.4 GHz RX** can serve simultaneously with its 433 MHz
  TX role would move Rank 3 up (a TCXO reference already fitted).
- A populated **NTC on the owned plain module** (deshield photo) would unblock Rank 2.
- A demonstration that an **AFC** or a carrier-offset readout *does* exist on the LR2021
  would replace D5 Rank 1's cycle-counter mechanism with a register read — and would change
  the open item that currently marks both as not found.

## 7. Notes

- **Why one record and not two.** The band-independence fact, the ladder correction and the
  Tx/Rx-budget correction are one decision — *what the FLRC drift strategy is, now that the
  flight mode is FLRC* — and they come from one analysis. Splitting them would leave the
  corrections orphaned from the fact that forces them.
- **Why the two existing records get appended corrections, not rewrites.** ADR-042 and
  ADR-056 each carry accepted-relevant work (heater rejection, TCXO-first, the NTC fix, GPS
  discipline) that this record does **not** supersede. Rewriting their bodies would erase
  that history; a correction section that states "your tolerance half is LoRa-based, FLRC
  governs, see ADR-057" keeps the history legible and the pointer findable. Their bodies are
  untouched.
- **Every quoted figure was re-verified, not inherited.** The FLRC tables were re-extracted
  from the in-tree AN1200.101 PDF (pp. 8–9) and the LR2021 Rev. 2.2 PDF (Tables 3-13/3-17,
  6-66/6-69/6-70) for this record; the F33 pin table (Rev. 1.1 §7) and the NiceRF `LoRa2021`
  pad table (V1.3 §7) were re-read. The SX1280 and MAX-M10S datasheets are **not vendored
  in-tree**; their facts are cited to `docs/analysis/external-clock-and-ocxo.md` §A3/§A5,
  which read them, and marked as such.
- **No hardware is ordered by this record.** It is design work only.

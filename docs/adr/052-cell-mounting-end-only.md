## 1. Context

### 1.1 The question the operator asked, and why it invalidated the previous answer

ADR-049 recommends a **spine-and-ribs** frame for the wings and rejects a full-area FR4
carrier on mass grounds. Following the earlier literature survey
(`docs/analysis/wing-literature-survey.md`), the assistant then recommended **downgrading**
that frame to a **"skeletonised carrier — a real substrate, bonded and vented, with windows
cut out"**, on the grounds that (i) no flight design in that survey carries bare cells on an
open frame and (ii) voids under a bond crack silicon in vacuum. That recommendation was
delivered to the operator on 2026-10-07.

**The operator challenged it on the correct grounds:** the mechanical rigour in that survey
came from **CubeSat** teams whose vehicles are ~1.33 kg, while his payload is ~20–40 g —
mass is roughly 30–60× more critical for him. He asked what **pico-balloon** builders
actually do. A dedicated survey was commissioned and answered the question from pico/small-balloon
sources only (`docs/analysis/pico-balloon-solar-survey.md`, 33 sources). **Its answer
supersedes the assistant's recommendation**, and this record encodes it.

### 1.2 The class, stated (so the comparison is bounded)

The survey defines a pico/small-balloon payload as one whose **entire launch mass** is under
about 100 g, with the tracker payload itself 10–30 g
(`docs/analysis/pico-balloon-solar-survey.md` §1). The operator's ~20–40 g payload sits at
the **heavy end of that band**, which is why the community's numbers are usable for him
rather than merely suggestive. The CubeSat comparator (a 1U vehicle at ~1.33 kg) is
**two orders of magnitude heavier** than his payload.

### 1.3 What no pico-balloon builder does

From the survey, verbatim:

> **"Nothing in the pico-balloon world bonds a bare cell face-down onto a full-area rigid
> substrate and vents it.** No source in this survey uses a \"bonded + vented substrate\" at
> all." — `docs/analysis/pico-balloon-solar-survey.md` §2.5

The three bare-cell builders found, and what each actually does:

| Builder | Attachment | Substrate | Bond |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Traquito** "Solar System" | solder the cell **ends** into a thin PCB strip | 0.6 mm FR4 strip, 5 mm wide (not full-area) | **none** |
| **NIBBB** "Standard Solar Array" | **glue the cell corners** to a foam plate | foam plate frame | corner glue only |
| **KC9IKB** (John Ruthroff) | **epoxy the array ends only**; cells float | Styrofoam airframe | **ends only, deliberate** |

The survey's summary table (§2.5) and its verdict (§3.2, §7 item 2) are unambiguous: the
pico-balloon world solves this by choosing a different **component** (an encapsulated
thin-film module) or by mounting the **bare cell at its ends only** — **not** by bonding a
bare cell to a vented rigid substrate.

## 2. Decision

### 2.1 Decision item 1 — cells are mounted by their ENDS ONLY; no adhesive bond, no full-face lamination

**Each cell is attached at its two ends only, and is otherwise free to move. There is no
adhesive bond between a cell face and a substrate, and no full-face lamination.** The mount
follows a pico-balloon pattern the survey records: the **Traquito PCB-sandwich** (solder the
cell ends into a thin PCB strip) or the **NIBBB foam-frame** pattern (corner attachment to a
foam plate). Cells must be free to move under thermal cycling.

**The rationale is THERMAL, not vacuum, and the survey names the builder who says so.** The
reason to avoid the bond is differential thermal contraction between the assembly temperature
and the flight cold, which can crack the cell — and the community's answer is to let the cells
float. The single most explicit statement found anywhere in the survey, from pico-balloon
builder John Ruthroff (KC9IKB), verbatim:

> *"Some people glue their cells to the airframe. I don't do this because of the possibility
> that the temperature change between the assembly area and the cold at 40,000 feet + may
> crack a cell. I just attach each end of the solar array to the airframe with epoxy, so the
> cells can 'float' and contract a certain amount."* — cited
> `docs/analysis/pico-balloon-solar-survey.md` §2.1 and §4.3 and §6 item 2

The survey states this is the **thermal-contraction** reason, "not the void reason — he avoids
the bond entirely rather than venting it" (§4.3), and it reports that **no pico/small-balloon
source read reports a cell cracked by trapped air expanding under a bonded cell** (§4.3, §6).

Corroborating attachment facts, verbatim from the survey (all a mounting-at-the-ends pattern,
none a full-face bond):

- **Traquito**: *"Solar System lets you build solar panels quickly and easily by sandwiching
  solar cells between two PCBs that do all the electrical connections for you."*; attachment
  is *"no need to use more than one oval per-side per-cell"*; the PCB strip *is* the structure.
  (§2.1)
- **NIBBB**: *"use the pattern to cut out the frame with the hobby knife from the middle of a
  8.825\" foam plate"* and *"Glue the panel to the frame using the corner holes for reference"*
  with *"Loctite Power Grab"* glue. (§2.1)

**Consequence for the design:** the wing's structural load path at 40 g is the **spine/rib or
the frame**, not a bonded film — and the cells are not a structural member. ADR-049's
spine-and-ribs frame already keeps cells out of the load path; this record keeps the cells
**unbonded** within it.

### 2.2 Decision item 2 — this SUPERSEDES the previously recommended bonded-and-vented skeletonised carrier

**The recommended "skeletonised carrier — a real substrate, bonded and vented, with windows
cut out" is SUPERSEDED.** Its CubeSat provenance made it the wrong comparator for a 20–40 g
payload, and no pico-balloon builder does it (§1.3).

**Where the superseded recommendation was recorded — named exactly, because it was never
committed to an ADR:**

1. **In-repo evidence base — `docs/analysis/wing-literature-survey.md` §5 lessons 1 and 2 and
   §7 A3.** Its §5.1 is titled *"Do not leave voids under the cell: in vacuum they crack the
   silicon."*, marked *"(APPLIES HERE — our most likely field failure.)"*, and says *"any bonded
   area must be void-free and vented"*; §5.2 is titled *"Bond the cell, but bond it sparingly
   and thin — and vent the bond line."*; §7 A3 is the *"Void/venting control"* item, adapted
   from AlbertaSat. **This is where the bonded-and-vented recommendation actually lives in the
   repo, and it is the CubeSat-derived text this ADR supersedes in part.**
2. **The recommendation to the operator, and the durable work-note.** It was delivered to the
   operator on 2026-10-07 (*"a skeletonised carrier — a real substrate, bonded and vented, with
   windows cut out where no copper is needed"*) and recorded the same day in the manager
   work-note log with the explicit statement *"NET EFFECT: ADR-049's spine-and-ribs
   recommendation must be DOWNGRADED to a SKELETONISED CARRIER — a bonded, vented substrate
   with windows cut out."* It was **never written into any ADR** — it was a proposed amendment
   to ADR-049 that never landed.
3. **The pico survey's name for it.** `docs/analysis/pico-balloon-solar-survey.md` §3.2 and
   §7 item 2 refer to the superseded position as *"ADR-049's bonded-and-vented substrate
   (inherited from the CubeSat survey)"*.

**Stated plainly, to keep the history legible:** **ADR-049's own committed text does not
recommend a bonded-and-vented substrate.** ADR-049 *rejects* the full-area FR4 carrier and
recommends the spine-and-ribs frame. The bonded-and-vented carrier was an assistant
recommendation *derived from* `docs/analysis/wing-literature-survey.md` §5 and *proposed as an
amendment to* ADR-049; that amendment was never written. This record closes the loop by
superseding the recommendation and pointing at where it came from, so a later reader who finds
"bonded and vented" in the literature survey can see it has been reversed.

**This record does NOT rewrite ADR-049.** ADR-049's spine-and-ribs structural decision, its
cell-class decision, and its ≥2 A / 40 V bypass-diode requirement stand; only the
**bond/venting** element is superseded, and only in part.

### 2.3 Decision item 3 — the CORRECTED flight environment; the earlier pressure figure was wrong

**The array's flight environment is 10–12 km ≈ 19–26 kPa (≈ 0.19–0.26 atm) at ≈ −50 to −56 °C,
for hours up to ≈ 300 days, day/night cycled.**

From the survey (§4.1–§4.2), on cited sources:

- QRP Labs / ZachTek: *"the electronics could survive during days and night in the cold of
  10 to 11 km altitude"* and *"most Pico balloons don't go over 11 km"*.
- US Standard Atmosphere (Engineering ToolBox, N/m² converted to kPa in the survey):
  10,000 m → **−49.9 °C**, 26.50 kPa; 15,000 m → −56.5 °C, 12.11 kPa.
  *Derived* in the survey: a payload at 10–12 km sits at **≈ 19–26 kPa ≈ 0.19–0.26 atm**, at
  **≈ −50 to −56 °C**.
- Duration: *"They can circle the earth multiple times, and stay in the air for hundreds of
  days."* (missile.design); QRP Labs: *"U4B-13 even went 17 times around the planet taking
  305 days"*.

**This explicitly corrects two earlier statements, both wrong, in the pessimistic direction:**

1. **The assistant's spoken figure to the operator on 2026-10-07** — *"1 atm → ~0.02 atm at
   altitude is 98% of the way to vacuum, so trapped air will expand and can crack silicon. The
   mechanism transfers to your flight."* **This figure is wrong.** 0.02 atm is ≈ 2 kPa, which
   is not the pressure at 10–12 km; the real ambient is 19–26 kPa, i.e. **0.19–0.26 atm**
   (survey §4.1). It is not "98 % of the way to vacuum"; it is roughly a **quarter** of surface
   pressure. The survey's own note on the committed *"~0.05–0.1 atm"* figure — that it "is
   closer to 15–18 km" — shows the same error direction: every candidate "wrong" figure is
   drawn from well **above** the pico-balloon float altitude, not at it.
2. **The committed figure in `docs/analysis/wing-literature-survey.md` §5.1** — *"our wing is
   bare cells on a spine/ribs frame at ~0.05–0.1 atm for hours and −60 °C"*. The survey notes
   this *"contradicts the earlier survey's '~0.05–0.1 atm'"* figure and that it is *"roughly
   half to a quarter of the real pressure at pico-balloon altitude"*, closer to 15–18 km
   (survey §4.1). The corrected environment above replaces it.

**And the void conclusion is NOT borrowed from CubeSat in either direction.** The survey
states, verbatim: *"I found no balloon-world evidence either way on void-cracking. I am not
borrowing the CubeSat conclusion."* (§4.4). The CubeSat void failure is a multi-year
< 10⁻⁶ Torr regime (AlbertaSat, TVAC at < 10⁻⁶ Torr ≈ 1.33 × 10⁻⁴ Pa) against a 12 km ambient
of ≈ 1.9 × 10⁴ Pa — a pressure ratio of **≈ 1.4 × 10⁸**. A void sealed at 1 atm on the ground
does see a real differential of ≈ 0.8 atm (≈ 81 kPa = 8.1 N/cm²) at altitude (survey §4.4),
which is why the end-only mount is prudent — but the **driving pressure, the gas available to
evolve from outgassing, and the thermal-cycle count are all in a different regime**, and no
balloon-world source either reports or fails to report void-cracking. **Recorded as an open
question, not as a transferred conclusion (§"Open items" 3).**

### 2.4 Decision item 4 — keep the bare polycrystalline cells; the measured W/g comparison decides it

**Keep the operator's bare polycrystalline cells. Do not switch the array to thin film.** The
measured W/g comparison decides it, and it is not close.

From the survey's §3.1 table and §3.3 (the operator's cell figures are the repo's own
measurements, *given*, not re-derived):

| System | Power | Mass | **W/g** | **mg/cm²** | Efficiency (derived) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operator's bare poly-Si cell (small) | 0.200 W | 0.50 g, 10.23 cm² | **0.400** | **48.9** | ≈ 19.6 % | repo measurement |
| PowerFilm **MPT6-150** | 0.600 W | 2.83 g, 166.44 cm² | **0.212** | **17.0** | ≈ 3.6 % | vendor |
| PowerFilm **MPT3.6-75** | 0.180 W | 2.14 g, 54.02 cm² | **0.084** | 39.6 | ≈ 3.3 % | vendor |

- **On W/g the bare silicon wins:** **0.400 W/g vs 0.212 W/g** for the best thin-film module
  found (PowerFilm MPT6-150, vendor) — **1.9× better per gram** — and **0.400 vs 0.084**
  against **MPT3.6-75**, the module the pico-balloon community actually recommends — **4.8×
  better** (survey §3.3 item 1, §5).
- **On areal mass the thin film wins, but it buys an area penalty:** **17.0 mg/cm²
  (MPT6-150) vs 48.9 mg/cm² (bare cell)** — 2.9× lighter per cm² — but at **≈ 3.6 % efficiency
  against the bare cell's ≈ 19.6 %**, so the mass saving buys a **5.4× area penalty**
  (survey §3.3 item 2, §5). For a mass-bound array the W/g column is the one that binds, and
  it favours silicon.
- **A full thin-film switch is unaffordable.** To replace the operator's 7.2 W from
  **367.2 cm²** of bare silicon (12 large cells × 30.6 cm²) with MPT6-150 modules would need
  **7.2 ÷ 0.6 = 12 modules = 33.96 g and 1997 cm²** (*derived*: 12 × 2.83 g, 12 × 166.44 cm²)
  — **heavier than the cells it replaces before any frame, and 5.4× the area**, which the
  survey states is *"unaffordable structurally and aerodynamically at 20–40 g"*
  (survey §3.3 item 4).

**Thin film is therefore reserved for the low-sun-angle / robustness case only — a mixed
strategy, not a wholesale switch.** The survey's §7 item 4 records the model: NIBBB itself
flies a bare-cell flat array in spring/summer and a thin-film prism in winter/Antarctic, and
KS4VA mounts PowerFilm for *"low sun angle"*. If a winter launch needs a low-angle blade, **one
encapsulated module** goes on it, not the whole array — with the survey's honest cost noted
(NIBBB's own thin-film low-sun array is **16.70 g for 0.816 W (0.049 W/g)** against the
bare-cell array's **4.6 g for 1.4 W (0.304 W/g)**, i.e. *"Three to four times heaver that
standard configuration"*).

### 2.5 Decision item 5 — one Schottky blocking/bypass diode per parallel group

**Add a Schottky (blocking/bypass) diode per parallel group**, as the balloon world does.
The survey (§7 item 5) records both balloon sources that face the dark-panel problem doing
this: KS4VA uses *"three SMD Schottky diodes … to 'steer' or isolate the dark solar panels
from the illuminated one"*, and NIBBB uses `1N5817` ×3. This is **consistent with ADR-049**,
which already requires a **≥2 A / 40 V** bypass per wing (SS24 or PMEG4020ER class, ADR-049
§"Consequences that change existing records" item 1). This record does not change the diode
rating; it records that the **per-parallel-group** placement is the community practice and is
retained.

### 2.6 Decision item 6 — the mass consequence: ≈ 0.15 g/cell mounting overhead, and the grams saved

**The balloon-world mounting overhead is tiny — ≈ 0.15 g per cell — so there is almost nothing
to save by changing the structure, and there is nothing to fear from removing the bonded
carrier.**

The survey derives it (§3.3 item 3, §7 item 3) from NIBBB's own measured array: **4.5–4.7 g
for 7 cells**, of which the 7 bare cells are ≈ 3.5 g using the operator's own 0.50 g/cell
figure. Therefore the *entire* foam-plate frame + glue + fibre-glass tape + 30 AWG wiring +
solder is **≈ 1.0–1.2 g for seven cells, about 0.15 g per cell (*derived*)**.

**Applied to 12 cells: 12 × 0.15 g = ≈ 1.8 g of mounting overhead.** Against the two figures
already committed in `docs/analysis/wing-mass-shape.md` §1.4:

| Structure (small-cell array, 12 cells) | Board / structural mass | Cells | Mounting **incl. wire + solder + glue** | Array total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) FULL CARRIER** (as-built) | 22.84 g FR4 (4 × 5.709) | 6.008 g | 0.36 g solder | **29.20 g** |
| **(b) SPINE + RIBS** (ADR-049) | 6.48 g FR4 (4 × 1.620) | 6.008 g | 0.36 g solder | **12.85 g** |
| **END-ONLY mount** (this record) | none (no substrate) | 6.008 g | **≈ 1.80 g** (12 × 0.15) | **≈ 7.81 g** |

**Grams saved.** Measured against the full carrier the end-only mount removes the entire
carrier: **29.20 g → ≈ 7.81 g, i.e. ≈ 21.4 g saved** on the array. Measured against ADR-049's
already-lightened spine-and-ribs array it saves a further **12.85 g → ≈ 7.81 g, i.e. ≈ 5.0 g**.
(Arithmetic: cells 6.008 g = 12 × 0.5006 g; FR4 masses are `docs/analysis/wing-mass-shape.md`
§1.4's own printed figures; mounting 12 × 0.15 g from the survey. The 0.15 g/cell figure
already includes the solder and wire allowance, so it is **not** added to the 0.36 g solder
line — the end-only row therefore carries no separate solder term.)

**`TODO(unverified)` — the 0.15 g/cell figure's basis must be confirmed before it is relied on
as a budget number.** It is a *derived* figure, not a measurement of the operator's assembly:
it assumes (i) NIBBB's 4.5–4.7 g array mass, (ii) the operator's own 0.50 g/cell for NIBBB's
52 × 19 mm AOSHIKE cells of the same class, and (iii) that the *difference* — frame + glue +
tape + wire + solder — is the mounting overhead. NIBBB does **not** publish a current figure for
its standard array without an internal contradiction (survey §3.3 item 5: "40 mA vs 400 mA"),
so the row cannot be cross-checked from the source. **Weigh the real as-built assembly (§"Open
items" 2) before using 1.8 g in a budget.** The end-only **direction** does not depend on the
exact figure — the full carrier is 22.84 g of FR4 and the wall is 0 g of substrate either way.

### 2.7 Decision item 7 — open items the OPERATOR must settle by bench test

These are the survey's §7.1 items. They are **not** decided here; this record recommends them
as the tests that would settle the open questions, and marks each as the operator's own work.
Design work only — **order nothing**.

1. **Cold-soak the soldered joints to ≈ −55 °C.** Whether the operator's hand-soldered joints
   survive −50 °C plus launch is untested, and the failure mechanism the community names is
   thermal. Survey §7.1 item 2: *"cold-soak a soldered cell string to ≈ −55 °C, thermal-cycle
   it, re-measure Isc and inspect the joints. There is no balloon source that has done this and
   published it."*
2. **Weigh the real as-built assembly.** Survey §7.1 item 1: assemble 7 (or 12) of the
   operator's cells end-only on a mock frame and **weigh + measure Isc at one sun**. This is
   the measurement that confirms or replaces the ≈ 0.15 g/cell overhead of §2.6.
3. **Measure the array current at 5°, 10° and 15° solar elevation.** The array must survive a
   winter northern launch; survey §7.1 item 4 states the low-sun-angle current is unmeasured
   and that NIBBB's answer was a physically different array while IEEE Spectrum's was to lose
   the flight. This is the measurement that tells the operator whether §2.4's mixed strategy
   (one encapsulated module for the low-sun case) is needed.
4. **Void-cracking under a bonded cell — no balloon evidence either way** (§2.3, survey §4.3).
   Recorded so that if a bonded wing is ever revisited, it is revisited as an *untested*
   option requiring the operator's own vacuum-bag or altitude-chamber cycle with a thermal
   camera — not as a settled requirement. **Explicitly NOT borrowed from CubeSat in either
   direction.**

## 3. Consequences

### Positive

- **The recommendation now rests on sources for the operator's own mass class**, not on a
  ~1.33 kg CubeSat comparator.
- **The mounting mass is ≈ 1.8 g for 12 cells** (§2.6) against 22.84 g of FR4 in the full
  carrier — the mass argument for the bonded carrier has no purchase.
- **The cracking mechanism the community actually observes is addressed** (thermal
  contraction, mitigated by letting the cells float end-only), instead of a vacuum mechanism
  for which no balloon-world evidence exists either way.
- **Consistent with ADR-049**: the spine-and-ribs load path, the cell class and the ≥2 A / 40 V
  diode requirement are all retained; only the bond/venting element is reversed.
- **Records the option that was NOT chosen** (the bonded-and-vented carrier) with its
  provenance, so a later session can reopen it without re-deriving it.

### Costs

- **Cells are exposed and fragile** — the survey's sources label bare cells *"Fragile"*
  (Traquito), *"Solar panels are very fragile"* (NIBBB), and *"Cracking and breaking of these
  incredibly fragile cells is par for the course"* (KC9IKB). Handling and launch damage is a
  real, sourced failure mode (AA0O broke cells at launch; survey §6).
- **The load path must come from the frame, not the cells** — an unbonded cell contributes
  nothing structural, so ADR-049's spine/ribs (or an equivalent frame) still carries the arm.
- **The end-only mount's own numbers are derived, not measured** (the 0.15 g/cell overhead,
  §2.6) — hence the bench tests in §2.7.
- **The earlier erroneous figures were published** to the operator (the "0.02 atm / 98 % of
  the way to vacuum" framing) and to the repo (`wing-literature-survey.md` §5.1's
  "~0.05–0.1 atm"); this record corrects both, and the correction is appended to both
  analysis documents (§Rollout) so the history stays legible.

## 1. Context

### 1.1 The four wings are ONE series string, wired through the hub

`docs/WING-TO-HUB-SOCKET-SPEC.md` §3.1 (branch `feat/wing-board-v9`), which ADR-046 §2.3 and
ADR-048 §2.3 reproduce and which the committed `v9_flight.net` implements:

```
stack_top  = W1_SOLAR_P   -> BAT54 -> supercap bank -> TPS7A02 (ADR-006)
             W1_SOLAR_N —(hub link)— W2_SOLAR_P
             W2_SOLAR_N —(hub link)— W3_SOLAR_P
             W3_SOLAR_N —(hub link)— W4_SOLAR_P
stack_bot  = W4_SOLAR_N   -> GND plane
```

So the array is **4 wings × 3 LARGE cells = 12 cells in ONE series string**, 6.0 V nominal
(ADR-006; ADR-049 §Decision). Cutting a wing at its tab removes that wing's three cells and
drops the string by 1.5 V. The converter floor is **2.5 V** (ADR-050 §3.2: the verified
best-fit TPS63060 is 2.5–12 V in; ADR-050 §4 states the practical floor is two wings = 3.0 V):

| Wings left | String V | Charging into the 5.4 V bank? |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 6.0 V | yes |
| 3 | 4.5 V | yes |
| 2 | 3.0 V | yes (the ADR-050 §3.2 practical floor) |
| 1 | 1.5 V | **no** — below the 2.5 V converter floor |
| 0 | 0.0 V | no — bank only |

(Arithmetic: 1.5 V per wing, ADR-006/ADR-049; floor 2.5 V, ADR-050 §3.2.)

### 1.2 Therefore the hub array is not an accessory — it is the enabling condition

The operator wants both a full-depth cut ladder **and** a vehicle that is still powered after
the wings are gone. §1.1 says the second cannot come from the wings. **The hub array is the
only thing that can close the survival requirement, and its *sizing* is therefore what decides
how deep the cut ladder may go** (§3.4).

### 1.3 The specific failure this record exists to prevent

If the hub array is wired **in series with**, or as a **tap on**, the wing chain, then the
**first cut removes the hub array's path together with the wing's cells** — one cut, and the
vehicle is on the bank alone (85.7 s at the 0.388 W average, ADR-050 §4). The cut then *causes*
the loss of the vehicle it was meant to keep alive. That is why the independence of the hub
string is written as a numbered decision (§2 item 1) **and** as a mechanical gate
(§2 item 1.3, `scripts/hub_array_topology_check.py`), not as prose.

### 1.4 The energy numbers, verified against the repo (and where the brief's framing is wrong)

**Cell nameplate power density.** ADR-049 §Measured inputs gives the LARGE cell as
78.55 × 38.90 × 0.21 mm, **30.6 cm²**, ~0.5 V, ~1.2 A. Therefore

```
P_cell  = 0.50 V × 1.20 A = 0.60 W
density = 0.60 W / 30.6 cm² = 19.608 mW/cm²      (face-on, AM0, nameplate)
```

**`19.6 mW/cm²` is the LARGE-CELL NAMEPLATE density, not a winter-insolation figure.** The
brief calls it "winter insolation 19.6 mW/cm²"; the repo has no such insolation number. The
same value also appears as the ADR-006 implied efficiency: 2.4 W / (1361 W/m² × 0.012 m²) =
14.7 % (`docs/SOLAR-PIN-REGULATORY.md` line 97) — i.e. **this cell is a ≈14.5 % class cell at
AM0 with no derate**, whereas `docs/SOLAR-PIN-REGULATORY.md` §1.1 derives an effective
**10.5 mW/cm²** for a 22 % cell *after* the repo's own 0.35 composite derate. **19.608
mW/cm² is an upper bound**, and §1.6 shows what happens when the derate chain is applied.

**The rotation factor.** The brief's `0.3051` is **verified** — but it is the value for cells in
**VERTICAL** planes:
`docs/analysis/wing-omnidirectional.md` §1.3 gives the vertical-blade rotation-average as
`cos e/π = 0.305107` at winter noon (`0.311486` daylight-mean), at 50 °N, δ = −23.44°
(assumed latitude/season — `TODO(unverified)`, §"Open items").

**And the hub plane is HORIZONTAL.** `docs/hardware-design.md` §3D-Assembly ("Hub-Board
**waagerecht** halten") is read by `docs/analysis/wing-insolation-geometry.md` §0 as: the hub
is horizontal, and the wing planes are perpendicular to it (**vertical**). A cell mounted flat
on the hub therefore sees the sun at `sin h`, not at the vertical-blade factor:

```
vertical-plane cell  : 19.608 × 0.305107 = 5.983 mW/cm² (noon) ; 0.311486 -> 6.108 (day-mean)
horizontal hub plane : 19.608 × 0.285020 = 5.589 mW/cm² (noon) ; 0.186530 -> 3.658 (day-mean)
```

`docs/analysis/wing-insolation-geometry.md` §2 gives these horizontal factors
(`sin h = 0.28502` at noon, rotation-independent; `<sin h> = 0.18653` day-mean).

**The average requirement.** 0.388 W — `docs/POWER-BUDGET-V9-D2BE.md` line 85 (the
representative daylight average load; ADR-050 §1 and §4 use the same number).

```
vertical  noon : 388 / 5.983  =  64.9 cm²      (the brief's number: 388/5.98 ≈ 65 cm²)
vertical  day  : 388 / 6.108  =  63.5 cm²
horizontal noon: 388 / 5.589  =  69.4 cm²
horizontal day : 388 / 3.658  = 106.1 cm²
```

**The brief's `~65 cm²` is correct only for cells in vertical planes.** Flush on the horizontal
hub plane — which is what "panels attached to the main hub board" literally means — the
day-mean requirement is **106.1 cm²**, i.e. **1.64×** the brief's figure. §1.6 adds the derate
chain on top.

### 1.5 Mass is the binding constraint, so the *delta* is what matters

ADR-049 §Decision: the payload is mass-bound, and its own headline saving (16.35 g) is the
whole point of the spine-and-ribs wing. 0.6 mm FR4 costs **127.6 mg/cm²**
(`docs/analysis/wing-mass-shape.md` lines 25/130) against **48.9 mg/cm²** for the silicon it
carries; a LARGE cell is **1.50 g** (ADR-049). So every square centimetre of hub array costs
`127.6 mg` of board plus the cells. The two candidate routes differ by **16.21 g** of board and
cells (§2 item 2), which is **1.26× ADR-049's entire 12.85 g four-wing array**.

### 1.6 Two independent derate chains — the sizing is sensitive to numbers nobody has measured

**Basis (A)** — nameplate density × the geometric rotation factor, then the repo's shading and
power-path derates, applied in the order the repo supplies them:

| Step | Source | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| nameplate × rotation, no other loss | §1.4 (vertical noon) | 64.9 cm² |
| × envelope shading 0.9 | `docs/SOLAR-PIN-REGULATORY.md` §1.1 (factor 0.9, "envelope and rigging") | 72.1 cm² |
| × charge-path efficiency 0.85 | ADR-050 §1 (the **assumed** 85 %; ADR-050 itself marks it `TODO(unverified)`) | **84.8 cm²** |
| horizontal hub plane, shading + 85 % | §1.4 horizontal day-mean | **138.7 cm²** |

**Basis (B)** — the repo's own composite derate. `docs/SOLAR-PIN-REGULATORY.md` §1.1 defines
`W/cm² = 1361 W/m² × η × 0.35 / 10000`, where the 0.35 already folds in incidence (≈0.5),
rotation (≈0.8) and envelope shading (≈0.9):

```
η = 0.1445 (this cell's implied AM0 efficiency: 0.60 W / 30.6 cm² / 1361 W/m²)
    -> 6.883 mW/cm² -> 56.4 cm² for 0.388 W
η = 0.22   (the document's optimistic assumption, a different cell class)
    -> 10.480 mW/cm² -> 37.0 cm²
```

**The two bases bracket the requirement at 56.4 – 84.8 cm²** on vertical planes, and **up to
138.7 cm²** if the cells are flush on the horizontal hub plane. They are two different models of
the same physics and they do not agree to better than ~1.5×; **neither the envelope-shading
factor (0.9) nor the charge-path efficiency (85 %) nor the cells' real MPP density is measured**,
and those three numbers move the answer by more than the difference between the candidate array
sizes. The array must therefore be sized with an explicit margin, and the shortfall stated rather
than hidden. **One measurement — the cells' MPP power density in flight illumination — would
collapse this whole section to a single number, and it is the highest-value measurement in the
record** (§"Open items" 6–7).

## 2. Decision

### 2.1 Decision item 1 — the hub array is an INDEPENDENT series string with its own converter input

**The hub-mounted array is its own series string. It is NEVER in series with the wing chain and
it is never a tap on it. Its positive terminal feeds its own converter/harvester input, on its
own net.**

1. **Mandated net names** (so a checker, a reviewer and the layout tool all mean the same thing):
   `HUB_PV_P` (the string's positive terminal and the harvester/converter input node),
   `HUB_PV_MID<n>` (inter-cell nodes inside the hub string), `HUB_PV_N` (the string's negative
   terminal). **Mandated cell references:** `PVA1 … PVAn`, one per hub cell, symbol
   `SolarCell_78x39mm` (the checker also accepts `HUB_C<n>`/`HUBPV<n>`/`HUB_SOLAR<n>` as aliases).
2. **The only node the hub string may share with the wing system is `GND`,** and only through
   `HUB_PV_N`. No `W<n>_SOLAR_P` and no `W<n>_SOLAR_N` land may appear on any `HUB_PV_*` net,
   and no `PVA<n>` terminal may sit on a net that carries a wing solar land.
3. **Enforcement — `scripts/hub_array_topology_check.py`.** A fail-closed parser of
   `v9_flight.net` (and, label-level only, of `v9_flight.kicad_sch`). It exits **1** if a hub
   cell terminal lands on a wing-chain net (rule R3b), or if the hub array's hot net shares a
   node with a wing land (R4), or if the hub array's return re-enters the wing string (R5); it
   exits **2** (never 0) when it cannot establish the answer. `tests/test_hub_array_topology.py`
   proves the gate passes a good fixture **and fails three deliberately-bad ones** (a tapped hub
   array, a series insertion between W2 and W3, a return inside the wing string). Run against
   the **shipped** v9 netlist it returns `UNDETERMINED (exit 2)` — correctly, because ADR-051
   is not implemented in the schematic yet:

   ```
   $ python3 scripts/hub_array_topology_check.py
     ok   R1 four wing interfaces present: J_W1, J_W2, J_W3, J_W4
     ok   R2 J_W1 pad 1 (SOLAR_P) -> net /W1_SOLAR_P        ... (8 lands, all exactly once)
     ok   R6 stack top J_W1.1 = /W1_SOLAR_P ; stack bottom J_W4.4 = GND
     ??   R3: no hub-array net found ... ADR-051 is not implemented in this schematic,
          so its electrical independence CANNOT be verified
   VERDICT: UNDETERMINED (exit 2)
   ```

4. **Consequence of getting this wrong, stated once and plainly:** the hub array becomes part
   of the string that the first cut severs. The cut then removes the vehicle's only remaining
   power source at the exact moment the cut is executed. **The cut becomes suicide.** There is
   no firmware recovery from it and no bypass that restores it.

### 2.2 Decision item 2 — hub array sizing and converter input: **2 large cells on a sub-µA harvester** (recommended), 5–6 cells on the main converter (rejected on mass)

**The requirement** (§1.4–1.6): 0.388 W average ⇒ **56.4–64.9 cm² on the most optimistic
vertical-plane bases, 84.8 cm² with the repo's own shading + 85 % power-path derates, and
106.1–138.7 cm² if the cells are flush on the horizontal hub plane.** One LARGE cell is 30.6 cm².

| Option | Cells | Area | String V | Closes 0.388 W? | Mass (cells + 0.6 mm FR4 board) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H2 (recommended)** | 2 LARGE | **61.2 cm²** | **1.0 V** | **94 %** of the optimistic 64.9 cm² basis; **58 %** of the horizontal day-mean basis — it does **not** fully close 0.388 W | 3.00 g + 7.81 g = **10.81 g** |
| H3 (fallback) | 3 LARGE | 91.8 cm² | 1.5 V | **142 %** of the optimistic 64.9 cm² basis (and 108 % of 84.8 cm²); **87 %** of the horizontal 106.1 cm² basis | 4.50 g + 11.71 g = **16.21 g** |
| M5 (main converter) | 5 LARGE | 153.0 cm² | **2.5 V** | yes on vertical planes; sits exactly on the 2.5 V floor (below) | 7.50 g + 19.52 g = **27.02 g** |
| M6 (main converter) | 6 LARGE | 183.6 cm² | 3.0 V | yes on any plane (173 % of the horizontal 106.1 cm²) | 9.00 g + 23.43 g = **32.43 g** |

**The key arithmetic, all computed from cited inputs:**

```
H2:  2 × 30.6 = 61.2 cm² ; 1.0 V  → 61.2 × 19.608 × 0.305107 (vertical) = 366 mW of the 388 mW
     61.2 × 19.608 × 0.186530 (horizontal day-mean)                    = 224 mW  (58 %)
     after the 0.9 shading and 0.85 power-path derates                = 280 mW  (72 %) vertical
                                                                        171 mW  (44 %) horizontal
H3:  3 × 30.6 = 91.8 cm² ; 1.5 V  → 549 mW (vertical) / 336 mW (horizontal day-mean)
     after 0.9 and 0.85                                               = 420 mW (108 %) / 257 mW (66 %)
M5:  5 × 30.6 = 153.0 cm²; 2.5 V  → the TPS63060's input MINIMUM exactly, ZERO margin
M6:  6 × 30.6 = 183.6 cm²; 3.0 V  → equals ADR-050 §3.2's practical two-wing floor

mass delta M5 − H2 = 3 cells (4.50 g) + 91.8 cm² × 127.6 mg/cm² (11.71 g) = 16.21 g
mass delta M6 − H2 = 3 cells (4.50 g) + 122.4 cm² × 127.6 mg/cm² (15.62 g) = 20.12 g
```

**Stated plainly: H2 does not fully close the 0.388 W average on any basis that includes the
derates** — 94 % optimistically and 72 % after shading and power-path loss. It is nonetheless the
**recommended** sizing because (i) it is the lightest option by 5.40 g against H3 and by 16.21 g
against M5, (ii) the derates it falls short of are unmeasured assumptions rather than
measurements, and (iii) a 6 % shortfall on the *average* is a slower duty cycle, not a lost
vehicle — whereas 16–20 g is a lost peak altitude. The honest formulation is: **H2 is the
sizing to build, H3 is the sizing to fall back to if the measurement lands badly, and the choice
between them is a bench result, not a preference** (§"Open items" 6–7).

**The mounting plane is therefore a first-class decision, not a detail.** If the hub-array cells
must lie flush on the **horizontal** hub plane — which is what "panels attached to the main hub
board" most naturally means (§1.4, §2.3) — then on the day-mean basis **neither H2 nor H3 closes
0.388 W** (58 % and 87 %), and closing it would need ≈**138.7 cm²**, i.e. **5 LARGE cells
(153.0 cm², 2.5 V, 27.02 g)** — which is H2's mass plus 16.21 g and lands back on the
main-converter option this record rejects. **The array is only small if its cells are vertical.**

**Decision: the hub array is 2 LARGE cells in series (61.2 cm², 1.0 V nominal) feeding a
dedicated sub-µA PV harvester into the bank — NOT the main converter.** Reasons:

- **1.0 V is below the main converter's 2.5 V floor, so a harvester is required regardless**
  of the cell count on this route. A harvester is not an extra: it is the input stage that makes
  2 cells possible at all.
- It is the lightest option (10.81 g) and it keeps the hub at the **energy-driven** size, which
  is ADR-049's stated preference and what the operator's mass constraint demands.
- The main-converter route costs **+16.21 g** (M5) or **+20.12 g** (M6) — **more than ADR-049's
  entire 12.85 g wing array** — to avoid a part that costs ≈1 g.
- **M5 is recorded as insufficient by inspection:** 5 cells = exactly 2.5 V, the converter's
  worst-case input minimum, with **zero margin** and no allowance for the MPP voltage dropping
  below open-circuit under load. If the main-converter route is chosen, the honest count is
  **M6 (6 cells, 3.0 V)** — the brief's "5 cells suffices" is *at the floor*, not above it.

**The harvester is a real part, and its figures are VERIFIED — with one correction and one
blocker.** Verified against the TI datasheet **bq25570, `SLUSBH2G`, March 2013 – revised March
2019 (Rev G), fetched from `https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq25570.pdf` on 2026-10-07**:

| Parameter | Datasheet value (Rev G) | Consequence here |
|---|---|---|
| Cold-start input voltage `VIN(CS)` | **TYP 600 mV, MAX 700 mV** | **CORRECTS the brief's "~330 mV".** Rev F *changed* this from 330 mV typical to 600 mV typical; Rev E raised `VIN(CS)` MAX from 450 mV to 700 mV (datasheet §4 Revision History). A 2-cell string at **1.0 V nominal** clears the 700 mV maximum by only **1.43×**, not the ~3× the 330 mV figure implied. **Acceptable, and stated as narrow.** |
| Continuous harvesting from `VIN` | down to **100 mV** | the 1.0 V string is well inside continuous-harvest range |
| `VIN(DC)` recommended operating | **0.1 V … 5.1 V** | 1.0 V fine; a 9.58 V cold string would NOT be |
| Full operating quiescent current | **488 nA typ** | vs ADR-050 §3.6's `I_Q ≤ 5 µA` bound → **met by 10×**; `488 nA × 5.4 V = 2.64 µW = 2.6 %` of ADR-036's 100 µW night anchor |
| Ship mode | < 5 nA from battery | acceptable disabled-state leakage under ADR-050 §3.6 |
| Minimum cold-start input power `PIN(CS)` | TYP **15 mW** | the array's day-mean 224–366 mW is 15–24× this |
| **Peak input power `PIN_PK`, absolute maximum** | **510 mW** | **← THE BLOCKER, below** |
| Recommended maximum input power | "**Maximum input power ≤ 400 mW.** Cold start has been completed" | idem |
| Package | VQFN-20, **3.50 × 3.50 mm**, mass not stated in the datasheet | ≈1 g class with its inductors and capacitors — `TODO(unverified)` until the passives are chosen |

**The blocker, quantified.** A 2-cell hub array is **61.2 cm²**, so its *face-on* output is

```
61.2 cm² × 19.608 mW/cm² × cos e (0.95852) = 1150 mW = 2.26 × the bq25570's 510 mW PIN_PK
```

**A 61.2 cm² array on a vertical-plane mount therefore exceeds the bq25570's absolute-maximum
input power by 2.26×**, and anything above 400 mW is outside the recommended range as well. The
harvester option is admissible **only** if the array's incident power is kept inside the part's
rating. Three admissible resolutions, none of which this record chooses unilaterally:

1. **Mount the hub-array cells on vertical faces** and accept that a *horizontal* array is what
   the 510 mW rating accommodates — on the horizontal hub plane at 50 °N winter noon the
   geometric peak is `61.2 × 19.608 × 0.28502` = **342 mW = 0.67 ×** the abs-max, i.e. inside
   rating. This trades area (106.1 vs 64.9 cm² day-mean) for headroom.
2. **Deliberately operate the array below its MPP** — the bq25570's programmable-MPPT
   input-voltage regulation can be set to hold `VIN_DC` at a low fraction of `Voc`, which caps
   the input power. This **throws away array power by design**, and must be recorded as such.
3. **Select a higher-input-power harvester.** Named candidates only, **no figure asserted**:
   LTC3105, SPV1040 — both are boost + MPPC front ends for low-voltage PV. Their cold-start
   voltage, quiescent current and peak input power are **`TODO(unverified)`**; downloading
   `analog.com` failed from this host on 2026-10-07, so nothing about them is claimed here.

**The recommendation is therefore CONDITIONAL, and the record says so.** H2 (2 cells, 61.2 cm²,
1.0 V on a sub-µA harvester) is the recommended sizing **if and only if** (a) the array's
incident peak stays inside the chosen harvester's input-power rating, and (b) the derate chain
of §1.6 is resolved by measurement in H2's favour. If either fails, the fallback is **H3
(3 cells, 91.8 cm², 1.5 V — still harvester-fed)**. If a harvester that tolerates the array
cannot be verified at all, then the hub array must shrink to fit the harvester (≈2 SMALL cells,
20.5 cm², 1.0 V, ≈401 mW face-on — inside the bq25570's 510 mW), and the vehicle will not run
the average from the hub array alone: **the cut ladder must then be capped at two cuts**
(§2.4).

### 2.3 Decision item 3 — the hub outline must grow: this is a FORM-FACTOR CHANGE, not a tweak

**The hub outline grows from 22 × 22 mm to a panel sized by the array.**

| | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Current (inherited) hub outline | **22 × 22 mm = 4.84 cm²** | CITED: `docs/hardware-design.md` line 13 ("Groesse: 22mm x 22mm"), restated ADR-046 §4.1, ADR-048 §2.1, socket spec §4. ADR-048 §5 notes **no v9 hub outline is drawn**. |
| Array area needed | **61.2 cm² (H2) … 106.1 cm² (horizontal day-mean)** | §1.4, §2.2 |
| Implied ratio | **12.6× (H2) … 21.9× (horizontal)** the 22 × 22 mm area | 61.2/4.84 ; 106.1/4.84 |
| Implied continuous single-face square | **78.2 × 78.2 mm (H2) … 103.0 × 103.0 mm** | √area |
| Practical outline incl. the four socket land rows, their 1.5 mm component keep-out (ADR-048 §2.4) and the component court | **≈ 90 × 90 mm (H2) … ≈ 115 × 115 mm** | derived; the dead bands are the socket rows at 4 edges |

This is stated as a **form-factor change** because it changes what the hub *is*: on 22 × 22 mm
a 61.2 cm² array **cannot be mounted at all** — the requirement exceeds the board by 12.6×. The
hub stops being the small mechanical centre the four wings converge on and becomes a panel that
carries cells as well. Consequences that must be re-frozen, not assumed:

- **ADR-046 §4.1 / ADR-048 §2.2–2.4 socket geometry**: the four 90° sockets, the 6.0 mm
  insertion depth, the slot keep-out and the ≥1.5 mm component keep-out all move to a new
  outline and must be re-placed.
- **ADR-030 (deterministic placement gate), ADR-040 (both radio sites on the back of the hub)
  and ADR-045 (antenna solder access)** now compete with the cells for the same copper and the
  same hand-soldering access. This is a **full re-layout of the v9 hub**, and ADR-030's
  placement gate must be re-run.
- **Single-face reality:** splitting the array across both hub faces does **not** halve the
  area. With a horizontal hub, the down-facing face receives no direct AM0 — only albedo, whose
  contribution is **not in any repo source → `TODO(unverified)`**. The array is therefore
  effectively **single-face** and the outline grows as computed above.
- `TODO(unverified)`: whether a 90–115 mm panel survives the mechanical loads that a 22 mm hub
  did (the four 176 mm cantilevered wings now load a much larger plate) — no in-repo source
  analyses it.

### 2.4 Decision item 4 — the cut topology and the electrical ladder

**The ladder is the interface between this record and the charge path. Cutting up to two wings
still leaves the wing string charging; the third and fourth cuts leave the vehicle on the hub
array alone.** Restating §1.1 as the decision:

| Cut depth | Wing string | Still charges? | Who is powering the vehicle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 cuts | 6.0 V | yes | the wing array (7.2 W peak — ADR-049) |
| 1 cut | 4.5 V | yes | the wing array, 3 wings |
| 2 cuts | 3.0 V | yes (at ADR-050 §3.2's floor) | the wing array, 2 wings |
| **3 cuts** | **1.5 V** | **NO** | **the hub array alone** |
| **4 cuts** | **0 V** | **NO** | **the hub array alone** |

**Decision 4.1 — the hub array's sizing (§2.2) is what determines whether a 3rd or 4th cut is
survivable.** The cut ladder's depth and the hub array's area are **one decision, not two**. If
the hub array covers only the most optimistic basis, the ladder must be **capped at two cuts**,
because cuts 3 and 4 put the vehicle on an array that cannot hold the average load. This is
recorded as a hard coupling: **no flight may carry four cutters without a hub array that closes
0.388 W on the basis the flight actually flies.**

**Decision 4.2 — the per-interface bypass diodes must be POPULATED, not DNP, on any flight that
carries cutters.** A cut leaves the wing's socket lands open, which **opens the string**; the
string continues only because the interface's bypass Schottky (D_BP1…D_BP4, across
`W<n>_SOLAR_P` ↔ `W<n>_SOLAR_N`, cathode to P) shorts the removed wing's position. So the bypass
provision is not a shading nicety for this flight — **it is the cut mechanism's other half.**
This **supersedes the DNP instruction** of ADR-046 §2.3/§6 and `docs/WING-TO-HUB-SOCKET-SPEC.md`
§5 for any flight carrying cutters. (ADR-046 §2.3 already mandates the provision; this record
mandates its *population* and its rating.)

**Decision 4.3 — the bypass path is a CONTINUOUS current path after a cut.** Post-cut, the
other three wings (up to 1.2 A, ADR-049) drive current **continuously** through the removed
wing's bypass diode, for the rest of the flight, not merely during a shading transient. The
diode's rating requirement is therefore a continuous-current requirement at altitude and cold
temperature — see item 7.

### 2.5 Decision item 5 — cutter actuation: nichrome per wing, MOSFET, **latched one-shot**

**One cutter channel per wing**, on the repo's own precedent: MOSFET (IRLML2502, ~0.02 g) +
nichrome wire + nylon ≈ **0.5 g per channel** (`docs/balloon-test-results.md` line 253) →
**4 channels ≈ 2.0 g** plus four nylon loops. Powered from the **bank node (VSCAP)** so it works
with the converter dead (ADR-047 §2.1: `VSCAP` is the raw supercap node, post-BAT54, pre-LDO).

1. **The drive must be a LATCHED one-shot.** Cutting is **irreversible**, and a level-sensitive
   driver can re-fire: a reset, a brown-out, a firmware retry or a floating gate would re-fire an
   already-fired channel. Each fire costs 2.59 J of the bank's 16.63 J usable energy
   (`docs/analysis/wing-jettison.md` §3.4) at the exact moment the bank matters. **Decision:** a
   firmware one-shot with a persistent (NVS) "fired" flag per channel, plus a hardware series
   gate that requires an explicit arming sequence, so that no single event — reset, retry or
   software defect — can fire a channel twice.
2. **Energy: ≈2.59 J per cut**, computed in `docs/analysis/wing-jettison.md` §3.2
   (`0.563 J` wire + `0.084 J` kerf, ×4 thermal-loss factor), which brackets the operator's
   **2–5 J** framing. All the thermophysical constants behind it are `TODO(unverified)` in that
   document and remain so here: the 2–5 J band is the honest planning range until measured.
3. **Sequencing — fires must not overlap.** Four simultaneous fires draw `4 × 2.49 A = 9.9 A`
   (at 5.4 V into a 2.17 Ω wire), which is **2.4× the IRLML2502's 4.2 A class rating** and sags
   the bank four times as hard (`docs/analysis/wing-jettison.md` §3.5). Firmware must serialise
   cuts with a minimum inter-cut interval.
4. **Preconditions at fire time.** `docs/analysis/wing-jettison.md` §3.4: the wire needs the raw
   bank node **above ≈3.3 V** (from an **assumed** 5 W loss floor, `TODO(unverified)`). If the
   balloon bursts at night the bank is near its 3.0 V floor and **the cut silently fails** — which
   is the benign failure, but it must be logged, not assumed.
5. **Cuts become cheap and repeatable, not a one-way spend.** With the hub array delivering the
   0.388 W average in daylight, a cut's energy refills in

   ```
   2.59 J / 0.388 W = 6.7 s      (the operator's 6–13 s band corresponds to the 2–5 J range:
                                  2 J -> 5.2 s ; 5 J -> 12.9 s)
   ```

   **This is the daylight figure only.** At night the hub array produces nothing and each cut is
   again a pure draw on the bank.
6. **Firmware safety limits.** A maximum on-time per channel (2.59 J at 13.4 W ≈ 193 ms; the
   ADR-050 §3.10 budget is **≤ 3.33 J = ≤ 10 % of the usable bank**), a hard inter-cut interval,
   a bank-voltage precondition, a maximum fired-channel count, and the latch of point 1.
7. **OPEN — the trigger mode is NOT decided.** Ground-commanded versus automatic. The operator
   has not decided, and this record **does not choose**. The two shapes are both recorded:
   *ground-commanded* needs a validated uplink at the moment of descent (and a failure mode where
   the uplink is lost); *automatic* needs a descent detector whose false-positive rate is
   unknown (a false positive at altitude is irreversible). Left open deliberately, in §"Open
   items".

### 2.6 Decision item 6 — `W<n>_RF` becomes the per-wing CUT-SENSE line

**`W<n>_RF` (the wing tab's pin 3, `RF_FEED`) is to be netted as the per-wing cut-sense /
continuity line**, so firmware can confirm that each cut fired and log it.

- **Verified current state:** pin 3 of `J_W1…J_W4` carries **no net** on v9 (ADR-046 §2.4,
  ADR-048 §2.3, socket spec §3.2), and the committed netlist confirms it: the four RF_FEED lands
  appear in no `(net …)`. This is a **free** provision — the connector position and the land
  already exist and are already being paid for.
- **The line:** per interface, `CUT_SENSE_W<n>`; on the hub, `W<n>_RF` → a series
  current-limiting resistor and a divider into a GPIO. GPIO budget is the constraint (ADR-029's
  free-GPIO audit; `OPEN-14` records IO12/IO47/IO48 unassigned): four dedicated GPIOs, or one
  GPIO plus a 4:1 analogue mux / resistor-ladder encoding, are both compatible with this record —
  the mechanism is fixed, the GPIO count is an open item.
- **Honest caveat — the sense line needs a WING change to be meaningful.** On the v9 wing,
  `RF_FEED` is a copper land with no net (ADR-046 §2.4: *"a copper land that is left electrically
  unconnected (a provision)"*). A continuity measurement to a pad that is floating on **both**
  ends proves nothing. For the cut-sense to actually sense, the **wing** must net its `RF_FEED`
  land into the cut loop's return (or the harness must carry the sense path independently). This
  is a **wing re-spin requirement**, recorded here and in §"Open items" as `TODO(unverified)`.
  Re-using pin 3 is free at the *connector*; it is not free at the *wing*.
- **ERC effect — verified count, not the brief's.** Netting pin 3 closes the four
  `pin_not_connected` errors at `Symbol J_W<n> Pin 3 [RF_FEED]`. The committed
  `tracker/hardware/schematics/flight_board/v9_flight-erc.rpt` reports, verbatim,
  **"ERC messages: 28  Errors 28  Warnings 0"**, of which exactly **4** are the wing RF_FEED
  lands. **The brief's "33 open `pin_not_connected` errors" does not match the committed report;
  the verified figure is 4 of 28.** The decision is unchanged either way: this closes 4.

### 2.7 Decision item 7 — re-rate the per-interface bypass diodes to ≥2 A / 40 V, and change their package

**The bypass diodes are re-rated from the current DNP BAT54 class (200 mA / 30 V) to ≥2 A / 40 V
per interface.** Two independent reasons, both from the repo:

1. **They were already undersized before any cut existed.** ADR-049 clause 1: "BAT54 family =
   200 mA / 30 V against the accepted 0.4 A array — undersized **before** any change. Re-rate to
   **≥2 A / 40 V** (SS24 or PMEG4020ER class)." The v9 array is a **1.2 A** LARGE-cell string
   (ADR-049), i.e. **6×** the BAT54's continuous rating at an open-circuit stack voltage of
   ≈9.58 V at −60 °C (ADR-050 §3.2) against a 30 V part.
2. **New requirement from this record: the post-cut path is CONTINUOUS** (§2.4 decision 4.3).
   After a cut, up to the array's full current flows through that interface's bypass diode for
   the rest of the flight. A continuous 1.2 A at ≥40 V is the requirement, sized with margin for
   the cold open-circuit case.

**Consequence:** SOD-323 (the socket spec §5 suggestion, chosen for a BAT54) **cannot carry 2 A**;
the land pattern must move to an SMA/SMB or leadless 2 A-class footprint. That is a **land
change on the hub**, and it re-opens ADR-048 §2's "one land row per interface" layout (the extra
footprint must be placed inside the interface keep-out without breaking ADR-046 §4's 1.5 mm
component clearance). `TODO(unverified)`: the temperature-derated continuous current of the
specific chosen part at altitude — the body diode's `I_F` falls with temperature; a cold
altitude flight helps, but the number is not in the repo.

### 2.8 Decision item 8 — cutting a wing does NOT lose any antenna: a RESOLVED NON-RISK

**Stated explicitly so the cut is not rejected on a comms objection it does not have.**

- **ADR-009 is Accepted** and chose **V1 omnidirectional wire dipoles mounted on the hub**:
  *"No SP4T switch, no antenna switching firmware, no **wing board antenna design**"*
  (verbatim, ADR-009 §Decision; the 868 MHz and 2.4 GHz dipoles are listed as hub-mounted, and
  ADR-009 §Consequences repeats "no wing board antenna design").
- **ADR-034 D1 and ADR-040 keep both v9 radio sites on the hub** (433 MHz TX, 2.4 GHz RX), and
  ADR-046 §2.4 confirms the wing's 2.4 GHz PCB Yagi *"is deferred to V2 and is NOT etched on the
  v9 wing"*.
- Therefore **cutting a wing removes solar cells, mass and drag — and nothing else.** No antenna,
  no feed, no radio function, no firmware mode. The comms link is unaffected by any cut.
- **Forward condition:** on **V2** (wing-borne PCB Yagis + SP4T, ADR-009 §V2), this stops being
  true, and the cut topology must be revisited before a V2 wing is flown with cutters.

## 3. Consequences

### Positive

- **The cut becomes survivable by construction.** The enabling condition the operator named is
  made explicit, given its own nets and its own converter input, and mechanically gated (§2.1.3).
- **Cuts become cheap and repeatable** in daylight: 6.7 s of hub-array output per 2.59 J cut
  (§2.5.5), instead of a one-way draw on a 16.63 J bank.
- **One new part class** (a sub-µA harvester + its passives, ≈1 g) instead of **+16.21 g** of
  cells and board (§2.2).
- **Four ERC `pin_not_connected` errors close** at zero connector cost (§2.6), and a new
  `CUT_SENSE_W<n>` telemetry line makes each cut **observable rather than assumed**.
- **The independence invariant is now machine-checked**, with a test that proves the check can
  fail (§2.1.3) — the failure mode is caught in CI, not in flight.

### Costs (accepted)

- **Mass — the price, and it is not small.** Hub cells 3.00 g (H2) or 4.50 g (H3); hub board area
  at 127.6 mg/cm² → 7.81 g (H2, 61.2 cm²) or 11.71 g (H3, 91.8 cm²); harvester ≈1 g; cutter
  channels 4 × 0.5 g ≈ 2.0 g (`docs/balloon-test-results.md` line 253); plus loops and harness.
  **Net new mass is of order 14–20 g on a payload whose binding constraint is mass** (ADR-049).
  ADR-049's entire four-wing array is 12.85 g; this record proposes to add more than that.
- **The hub is re-laid-out.** A 12.6×–21.9× area increase is a **form-factor change** (§2.3),
  re-freezing ADR-030's placement gate, ADR-040's radio site, ADR-045's solder access and
  ADR-046 §4 / ADR-048 §2's socket geometry.
- **A new failure point:** a single tap/series wiring mistake silently converts the cut into
  suicide (§2.1.4). It is caught only if `scripts/hub_array_topology_check.py` is wired into the
  flight-board CI.
- **The cut-sense needs a wing re-spin** to be meaningful; pin 3 is floating on the v9 wing
  (§2.6).
- **The harvester adds a quiescent line** (488 nA = 2.64 µW = 2.6 % of ADR-036's 100 µW night
  anchor) and a switching EMI source beside the receiver — the same question ADR-050 §3.9 leaves
  to the bench, and the answer is not assumed here.
- **A new input-power hazard:** an array whose face-on output exceeds the harvester's rating
  (§2.2, the bq25570's 510 mW `PIN_PK` absolved only by a horizontal mount or a deliberate
  off-MPP limit).
- **H2's coverage falls short of its own requirement:** 94 % of the most optimistic basis, and
  **72 %** after the shading and power-path derates (280 mW of the 388 mW needed; 44 % on the
  horizontal plane). Recorded as short, not as closed — the shape of the fix is either H3
  (+5.40 g) or a slower duty cycle, and the choice is a bench result (§1.6, §2.2).

The DNP instruction survives only for a cutter-less flight. |

## 4. Open items (all explicit, none invented)

1. **Cutter trigger mode — ground-commanded vs automatic.** The operator has not decided; this
   record does not choose (§2.5.7).
2. **The hub outline decision.** ≈90 × 90 mm (H2) to ≈115 × 115 mm, versus enlarging the hub,
   versus mounting the cells on vertical faces of a 3-D hub collar, versus accepting a shorter
   cut ladder (§2.3).
3. **Whether the hub array uses the sub-µA harvester (H2/H3) or the main converter (M5/M6)**
   (§2.2).
4. **The hub-array cell mounting plane** — vertical faces (0.3051 factor) versus flush on the
   horizontal hub plane (0.1865 day-mean). **Load-bearing for the area** (§1.4, §2.3).
5. **The selected harvester's peak-input-power rating** versus the array's face-on peak
   (bq25570: 510 mW absolved vs 1150 mW at a vertical 61.2 cm² mount) — and the figures for the
   named alternatives LTC3105 / SPV1040, all **`TODO(unverified)`** (analog.com was unreachable
   from this host on 2026-10-07) (§2.2).
6. **The cells' real MPP power density** — 19.608 mW/cm² is a nameplate upper bound, derived
   from the cell's 0.5 V × 1.2 A over 30.6 cm². The two repo-derived bases bracket the
   requirement at **56.4 – 84.8 cm²** (vertical planes) and up to **138.7 cm²** (horizontal
   plane), and only a measurement can say which end of that range is real (§1.4, §1.6). **This
   single measurement decides the record's sizing.**
7. **The envelope-shading derate (0.9) and the charge-path efficiency (85 %)** — both assumed,
   both `TODO(unverified)` in their own sources, and together they move the requirement from
   64.9 to 84.8 cm² (§1.6).
8. **Cut energy** — the repo's 2.59 J is a computation with `TODO(unverified)` thermophysical
   constants; the operator's 2–5 J band is the planning range (§2.5.2). Also open: the loss
   floor (assumed 5 W) that sets the ≈3.3 V arming threshold (§2.5.4).
9. **Wing re-spin to net `RF_FEED` into the cut loop**, without which the cut-sense senses a
   floating pad (§2.6).
10. **GPIO assignment for `CUT_SENSE_W<n>`** against ADR-029's free-GPIO budget (`OPEN-14`) —
    four GPIOs, or one plus a mux/ladder (§2.6).
11. **The chosen bypass diode's continuous current at altitude/cold temperature**, and the
    package/land change it forces inside the interface keep-out (§2.7).
12. **Albedo contribution of the hub's down-facing face**, which is what would make a two-face
    array legitimate (§2.3).
13. **Launch latitude and season** — the insolation factors assume 50.0 °N / winter solstice
    (`docs/analysis/wing-insolation-geometry.md` §1, §7: the repo states neither).
14. **Mechanical adequacy of a 90–115 mm hub panel** under four cantilevered 176 mm wings
    (§2.3).
15. **Harvester mass with its passives**, and its EMI verdict beside the receiver (§3).

## 5. What would falsify this

- A measured cell MPP density materially below 19.608 mW/cm² **and** no shading headroom →
  the H2/H3 sizing does not close 0.388 W, the hub array must either be abandoned or the cut
  ladder capped at two cuts (§2.4 decision 4.1 must be amended with the number).
- A measured cut energy above 3.33 J that cannot be reduced → ADR-050 §3.10's budget is
  breached and the fix is a mechanical/spring release, which re-opens the cut mechanism
  (`docs/analysis/wing-jettison.md` §4).
- A harvester found that tolerates the array's face-on peak **and** cold-starts below 0.8 V
  with a sub-µA quiescent → §2.2's conditionality can be lifted and H2 written as
  unconditional.
- A bench measurement showing the batched `CUT_SENSE_W<n>` line is not readable through the
  cut (e.g. the loop's resistance is not distinguishably open after the cut) → §2.6's detection
  claim is wrong and the sense method changes, though the pin-3 provision remains free.
- A measured 2.59 J cut that the hub array cannot refill within the daylight pass → §2.5.5's
  "cheap and repeatable" claim is downgraded to "affordable once".
- Evidence that the hub cannot be enlarged without breaking ADR-045's solder access or ADR-030's
  placement gate → the array must move to a 3-D hub collar, and §2.3 is amended.

## 7. Notes

- **Why the independence rule is written twice** (§2.1 decision and §2.1.3 checker): it is the
  one decision in this record that makes an irreversible in-flight action safe, and prose does
  not fail a build. The checker exists so the rule can *fail a build*.
- **Why the brief's `0.3051` is kept but qualified.** It is a real, cited number — for vertical
  planes. The hub plane is horizontal (`docs/hardware-design.md` §3D-Assembly), so the record
  gives both factors rather than silently using the flattering one (§1.4).
- **Why the `33 ERC errors` figure is not repeated.** The committed report says 28 errors and 4
  of them are the wing lands (§2.6). Restating an unverified count would be exactly the kind of
  drift this corpus is careful about.
- **Why the bq25570's 600 mV cold-start is recorded even though 1.0 V clears it.** The brief
  cited ~330 mV; the datasheet changed it to 600 mV in Rev F. A design that assumes 3× margin
  when it has 1.43× is a different design, and the difference is the sort of thing that is only
  ever caught by reading the revision history.
- **In-flight work not duplicated:** the wing jettison trade study (`analysis/wing-jettison`,
  recommend-do-nothing on the *single-cut whole-array* proposal) and the progressive-shed ladder
  (`docs/analysis/wing-ladder.md`, `REJECT`) both priced the cut against a vehicle that had no
  hub array. **This record changes their premise**: with the hub array fitted, the objection
  "the mechanism needs its own victim to power its execution" (wing-jettison §3.4) is answered
  for daylight operation, and both studies should be re-read against §2.5 before the cut is
  finally accepted or rejected. This record does **not** re-run them.
- **No hardware is ordered by this record.** It is design work only.

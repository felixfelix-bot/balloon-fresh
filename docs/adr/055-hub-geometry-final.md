## 1. Context (the evidence, verified against the repo)

### 1.1 The orientation fork the repo already framed

Two geometry analyses already put the numbers on the table, and they disagree on the answer while
agreeing on the physics:

- **The insolation analysis** derives that the wing planes are **VERTICAL** (four near-vertical
  blades) and ranks vertical first on the **series, whole-day** metric: **0.25589 vertical vs
  0.18629 horizontal = 1.374× (+37 % daily)**, while conceding the flat cross is **13.7 % better
  at noon** on the same series metric (`docs/analysis/wing-insolation-geometry.md` §2b, §5).
- **ADR-049** rejected the "flat horizontal cross" on the **geometric** 4-arm metric:
  **0.1863 (horizontal day) vs 0.2559 (vertical)** — "vertical is +37 %"
  (`docs/adr/049-wing-architecture.md` §"Why each rejected option was rejected").
- **ADR-051** then sized the *hub* array and found the plane is a **first-class** decision: on
  the horizontal plane the day-mean requirement is **106.1 cm²**, i.e. **1.64×** the
  vertical-plane 64.9 cm² figure (`docs/adr/051-hub-array-and-cut-topology.md` §1.4).

So the repo's own numbers say the horizontal plane is *larger* for the same energy. This record
**accepts the larger plane anyway**, and the reasoning is below — it is a *robustness* reasoning,
not an efficiency one.

### 1.2 The rotation averages, verified

From `docs/analysis/wing-insolation-geometry.md` §2 and §2b (reproducible via
`python3 docs/analysis/insolation_factors.py`):

```
single vertical blade, rotation-average   <F>₁ = cos h / π = 0.30511   (day-mean 0.31149)
single horizontal plane, rotation-average <F>₁ = sin h     = 0.28502   (day-mean 0.18653)
day-mean ratio vertical / horizontal      = 1.6699                    (+67 %)
```

and the **series (as-wired)** metric over the whole day: **vertical 0.25589 vs horizontal
0.18629 = 1.374×** (§2b). The **1.64×** figure this record uses for "vertical is better per unit
area, day-mean" is **0.30511 / 0.18653 = 1.6357** — the ratio of the *per-unit-area* day-mean
factors, and it is the same 1.64 that ADR-051 §1.4 prints (64.9 → 106.1 cm²).

### 1.3 The ripple figure, verified

From `docs/analysis/wing-ngon-prisms.md` §2.1 (reproducible via
`python3 docs/analysis/ngon_prisms.py`): the **N = 4 square of vertical faces carries a peak-to-
peak ripple of 32.53 % about its mean** (±16.27 %), worse than N = 3 (14.03 %) and N = 5 (4.98 %).
A **horizontal plane has no ripple at all** — `cos(incidence) = sin h` is **independent of the
rotation angle ψ** (`docs/analysis/wing-insolation-geometry.md` §2: "the factor is `sin h` at
every rotation angle — genuinely rotation-independent"; N-gon analysis §2.4).
**Zero versus 32.5 %: this is the single sharpest number in the orientation fork.**

### 1.4 The winter sun, verified

At the assumed site/season (**50.0 °N, winter solstice, δ = −23.44°** — assumed, and
`TODO(unverified)`, no in-repo source states latitude or season):

```
noon solar elevation h = 90 − |50.0 − (−23.44)| = 16.56°   (≈ 17°)
horizontal-plane factor at noon = sin h = 0.28502 ≈ 0.286
day length = 7.85 h
```

(`docs/analysis/wing-insolation-geometry.md` §1.) The sun **never rises above ≈17° all winter
day** at 50 °N; a horizontal plane is therefore **maximally ~0.286 of face-on even at noon**.

### 1.5 The mass levers, verified

From `docs/analysis/wing-mass-shape.md` §2.1 and `docs/adr/051-hub-array-and-cut-topology.md`
§1.5:

| Board | Areal mass | At 106.1 cm² |
|---|---|---|
| 0.6 mm FR4 | **127.65 mg/cm²** (`0.060 cm × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.15`) | **13.54 g ≈ 13.5 g** |
| 0.4 mm FR4 | **85.1 mg/cm²** (`wing-mass-shape.md` §2.1) | **9.03 g ≈ 9.0 g** |

A 0.4 mm board therefore saves **≈4.5 g** at the 106 cm² target — **larger than any other single
lever left on the hub**.

### 1.6 The crowding figure, verified

`docs/WING-TO-HUB-SOCKET-SPEC.md` §4, "Slot-to-slot": *"on a 22 × 22 mm hub an **8 mm-wide tab
centred on a 22 mm edge leaves 7 mm per side** — no interference, but the routed slot length

and it does not change the direction.)

### 1.7 The end-only mount, verified

`docs/adr/052-cell-mounting-end-only.md` §2.1: cells attach **at their two ends only**, with **no
adhesive bond** and no full-face lamination, following the Traquito PCB-sandwich or NIBBB
foam-frame pico-balloon pattern. The rationale is **thermal contraction**, not vacuum.

## 2. Decision

### D1. ORIENTATION: HORIZONTAL, flat, no tilt.

**The hub array lies in a HORIZONTAL plane — flat, un-tilted.** This is a deliberate reversal of
ADR-049's "flat horizontal cross — REJECT" line and of the insolation analysis's ranking, and the
reasoning is **ranked**, because more than one argument carries it:

**(a) Rotation invariance gives ZERO ripple, versus 32.5 % for a four-face vertical arrangement
— and the governing principle is 'degrade rather than lose'.**
A four-face vertical cross (the N = 4 square of the N-gon analysis) swings **32.53 % peak-to-peak**
about its mean as the vehicle rotates (±16.27 %; `docs/analysis/wing-ngon-prisms.md` §2.1, §2.2).
A horizontal plane does **not move at all**: its factor is `sin h` at every rotation angle
(`wing-insolation-geometry.md` §2). **Zero ripple versus 32.5 %.** On a vehicle whose governing
principle is **degrade rather than lose the balloon**, a **steady** array beats a **bigger** one,
because the guarded failure mode is a **dip**, and a dip is exactly what the bank cannot cover if
it arrives when the bank is already drawn down. A steady array has a **predictable** worst case;
a rotating one hands the vehicle a 32.5 % amplitude it must ride through every turn, forever.

**(b) The vehicle has no attitude control and rotates slowly.** Any oriented geometry (blades,
fins) relies on **averaging over a full turn** and therefore **spends long stretches badly
oriented** — and the N-gon analysis shows the vertical cross is **exactly edge-on to two of its
four faces four times per rotation** (`wing-ngon-prisms.md` §2.2: at ψ = 0 the perpendicular
faces are "exactly edge-on (0.000)"). A horizontal plane has **no bad stretch** — every rotation
angle is the same angle — **only a bad season**, and a season is **predictable and designable**
(sin h is known for the launch date; the array is sized for it, D3). Design against a season you
can compute, not against a rotation phase you cannot.

**(c) Buildability — and this is decisive on a hand-soldered first flight.** Cells are **0.21 mm
wafers mounted END-ONLY with NO ADHESIVE BOND** (ADR-052 §2.1). Mounting a bare wafer at its two
ends with no bonded face is **trivial flat in the board plane**: the board *is* the carrier, the
workholding is a flat table, and the cell ends land on pads beside it. To do the same on four
vertical fins means solving the **same unbonded end-mount four times over**, in four planes, plus
**four mechanical interfaces** (ADR-046/ADR-048 socket land sets) and **four planes that must be
aligned** to each other. That is four times the assembly risk on a payload that is
**hand-soldered**. The flat plane makes the hardest operation in the whole array (attach a
0.21 mm wafer with no glue) the easiest one.

**(d) Mass is a WASH — this is NOT a mass decision, and the earlier ≈11 g claim is RETRACTED as
WRONG.**
- **Horizontal costs board the fins do not need.** To hold the same day-mean energy, the
  horizontal plane needs **106.1 cm²** where the vertical plane needs **64.9 cm²** (ADR-051 §1.4)
  — a **delta of 41.2 cm²**. At **127.6 mg/cm²** (0.6 mm FR4, `wing-mass-shape.md` §2.1) that is
  **41.2 × 0.1276 = 5.26 g ≈ +5.3 g of board**. (41.2 cm² = 106.1 − 64.9, the two figures
  ADR-051 §1.4 prints.)
- **Fins save that board but add four structural planes and four interfaces.** The +5.3 g is not
  a win for either side; it is the price of the steady array, and it buys nothing on mass — the
  fins would pay it back in four structural planes and four socket joints.
- **Stated plainly, so no reader infers a mass motive:** **this record does not choose horizontal
  because it is lighter. It is not lighter.** The orientation decision is made under (a), (b) and
  (c).
- **CORRECTION, carried explicitly.** An **earlier manager claim was that the end-only mounting
  change freed about ≈11 g** of array mass. **That claim is WRONG and is RETRACTED here.** It was
  a spoken/working figure and appears in **no committed document**; the **corrected, committed
  figure is `docs/adr/052-cell-mounting-end-only.md` §2.6: the end-only mount saves ≈21.4 g
  against the full carrier but only ≈5.0 g against ADR-049's already-lightened spine-and-ribs
  array** (12.85 g → ≈7.81 g). Anyone budgeting on "≈11 g" is budgeting on a number that does
  not exist.

**(e) The manager's earlier suspension-line-shadow objection is RETRACTED as OVERSTATED.**
An earlier manager concern held that the suspension line's shadow would materially cost the
horizontal array. **It is retracted.** A **30 AWG** line is **0.255 mm** in diameter, so its
shadow is a **thin stripe**, not a blanking occluder; and a shadowed cell is a case the vehicle
already handles — **the hub-side per-interface bypass diodes** (ADR-053, confirmed mandatory and
load-bearing in ADR-054 §2.2/§3.2) short the affected position and keep the string charging.
**The objection does not survive contact with the diode provision it ignores.** (D6 removes even
the stripe.)

### D2. The honest cost of horizontal, stated as a SPECIFICATION, not hidden.

**A horizontal plane tracks `sin(elevation)`. At 50 °N in winter the sun never exceeds ≈17°, so
instantaneous output peaks at ≈0.286 of face-on even at noon** (`sin 16.56° = 0.28502`;
`wing-insolation-geometry.md` §1). Over the whole day the **day-mean factor is 0.18653**, against
the vertical blade's **0.30511** — **vertical fins are ≈1.64× better per unit area, day-mean**
(0.30511 / 0.18653 = 1.6357; §1.2).

Therefore the horizontal array is **larger** (D3) **and** runs at **low instantaneous output**
(≤0.286 of face-on). **This makes it a HARD REQUIREMENT, not a preference, that the charge
converter performs well at LOW INPUT VOLTAGE.**

> **CONVERTER SPECIFICATION (recorded here as a requirement):** the hub-array charge converter
> **must cold-start and harvest efficiently at low input voltage** — a sub-µA-class PV harvester
> with a low cold-start threshold, not a converter that only works near the array's open-circuit
> voltage. The design reference is the **bq25570** verified in ADR-051 §2.2 (cold-start
> `VIN(CS)` **TYP 600 mV / MAX 700 mV**, continuous harvesting down to **100 mV**, full-operating
> quiescent **488 nA typ**). A converter whose input floor is high enough that it is idle for
> most of the winter day **defeats the horizontal choice** and must not be selected.

The reduced input voltage is a **lower bound on converter input range**, and it is
`TODO(unverified)` only in the sense that no in-repo source has *measured* the loaded `Vmp` of the
hub cells at 0.286 illumination — the *geometry* (0.286) is computed and the harvester's
low-voltage capability is datasheet-verified.

### D3. The array AREA is a DUTY CHOICE, not a requirement.

**The 106.1 cm² figure is a CEILING, not a floor.** It exists for exactly one reason: to cover the
**full-design 0.388 W average load** (`docs/POWER-BUDGET-V9-D2BE.md` line 85:
`(0.159 W + 0.164 W) × 1.20 = 0.388 W`) — the **F33-driven** figure (the `LoRa2021F33-2G4` 1 W PA
is the dominant 5 V-rail load in that budget). Because **"degrade rather than lose the balloon"
is the governing principle**, array size is the knob that sets **the sustained TX duty the vehicle
can hold forever**. Harvestable day-mean energy scales **linearly with installed area**, so duty
does too, against the fixed full-design load:

| Hub-array area | Sustained duty it can hold (vs full design) |
|---|---|
| **≈106 cm²** | **full duty** (the 0.388 W full design) |
| **≈80 cm²** | **≈75 % duty** |
| **≈53 cm²** | **≈50 % duty** |

(The ratio is `area / 106.1`: 80/106.1 = 75.4 %; 53/106.1 = 50.0 %.)

**State plainly: the operator must choose the sustained duty, and this ADR does not choose it for
him.** A smaller, cheaper, lighter hub that transmits half the time is a **legitimate vehicle**,
not a degraded one — that is the whole point of "degrade rather than lose". The area is the
expression of the duty decision, and the duty decision is his (§"Open items" 1).

### D4. Board thickness is the largest remaining mass lever and is to be MINIMISED.

**At 106 cm²: 0.6 mm FR4 is ≈13.5 g; 0.4 mm FR4 is ≈9.0 g — a ≈4.5 g saving** (§1.5:
106.1 cm² × 127.65 mg/cm² = 13.54 g; × 85.1 mg/cm² = 9.03 g). **That 4.5 g is LARGER than any
other single lever left on the hub, and much larger than the orientation question** (which, per
D1(d), is not a mass lever at all, only a +5.3 g board delta between two orientations).

**Decision: 0.4 mm FR4 is the TARGET hub thickness** — the thickness `docs/hardware-design.md`
line 14 already floats ("oder 0.4mm fuer Gewichtsoptimierung"), and the one `wing-mass-shape.md`
§2.1 quantifies at **85.1 mg/cm²**.

**The stiffness trade is noted**: a 0.4 mm plate is less stiff than 0.6 mm, and the four
≈176 mm cantilevered wings now load a larger plate (ADR-051 §2.3 already flags the mechanical
adequacy of the grown panel as unanalysed). **A stack-up / stiffness check is REQUIRED before
0.4 mm is frozen** (§"Open items" 3) — a lighter board that warps or flexes the cells off their
end-only joints is not a saving.

### D5. The hub grows to ≈103 mm square for 106 cm² — and the growth REMOVES a real crowding problem.

**Arithmetic: 106.1 cm² implies a continuous single-face square of √106.1 cm² ≈ 103 mm, i.e.
≈103 × 103 mm** (ADR-051 §2.3 states the implied continuous shared square as **103.0 × 103.0 mm**
for the horizontal case).

**The growth is not purely a cost — it removes a real crowding problem.** `docs/WING-TO-HUB-
SOCKET-SPEC.md` §4 flags that **"on a 22 × 22 mm hub an 8 mm-wide tab centred on a 22 mm edge
leaves only 7 mm per side"** on today's inherited outline. Growing the hub to ≈103 mm square turns
that 7 mm-per-side margin into a comfortable one and gives the four socket land rows, their
1.5 mm component keep-out and the component court room to coexist with the array.

**Square is preferred** on two grounds: (i) it matches the **four 90-degree socket symmetry**
(ADR-046/ADR-048's four interfaces at 90° spacing) without a preferential axis; (ii) **a square
maximises area per perimeter** among rectangles of a given diagonal, minimising the outline the
four wings must span.

### D6. Mounting: cells on the UPPER face, attachment RAISED on a standoff.

**The hub-array cells are mounted on the UPPER face of the board**, and the **suspension
attachment is raised on a standoff above the array** so the line and its **shadow cone clear the
array**. This is the cheap, mechanical removal of the shadow concern that D1(e) already retracts on
the diode-handling grounds — with the attachment raised, the line's shadow lands **off** the array
for a segment above it, and the thin 30 AWG stripe never crosses a producing cell. (The
balloon/shadow geometry of `docs/analysis/wing-omnidirectional.md` §4 — the shadow centre thrown
`cot e × Z = 3.363 Z` away — is what the standoff height is chosen against; the exact standoff is a
layout number, `TODO(unverified)`.)

**Upper face, not both faces:** with a horizontal hub the **down-facing face receives no direct
AM0** — only albedo, whose contribution is **not in any repo source** (ADR-051 §2.3). The array is
therefore effectively **single-face**, and "upper face" is the face that sees the sun.

### D7. A separate array carrier versus one integrated board is an OPEN question, not decided.

**This is recorded as a question to be COSTED, not decided.**
- The **radios need ≈25 cm²** (`TODO(unverified)` — this figure is the operator/manager-stated
  component-court area; **no in-repo source states it**, so it is flagged, not asserted) and the
  **array needs ≈106 cm²**.
- **Conflating them forces the full 13.5 g plate** (D4): if the array and the electronics share
  one 106 cm² board, every square centimetre of that board is paid for at the full 106.1 cm²
  areal cost, including the ≈25 cm² that carries no cells.
- **A small, dense electronics board plus a thin separate array carrier could be lighter** — the
  electronics board shrinks to its own ≈25 cm², and the array carrier is optimised for the
  end-only cells — **at the cost of one interface connector and one more failure point** (a
  connector that can fail, and a second board that must be mechanically referenced to the first).
- **Cost it; do not decide it.** The trade is (grams saved by not paying FR4 under the
  electronics) versus (connector mass + one added failure mode + one added interface). It is an
  **Open item** (§"Open items" 4).

## 4. Open items

1. **THE SUSTAINED-DUTY CHOICE (the operator's, D3).** The operator must choose the sustained TX
   duty the vehicle must hold (full / ≈75 % / ≈50 %), which sets the array area (≈106 / ≈80 /
   ≈53 cm²). **This ADR does not choose it.** Until it is chosen the array area is a **ceiling**,
   not a frozen number.

2. **THE HUB-OUTLINE CONTRADICTION (pending, another task's job).** 22 × 22 mm (4.84 cm²) versus
   55.15 × 45.15 mm (24.90 cm²). Recorded as a **KNOWN DEPENDENCY** (§3); this record does **not**
   pick a winner. It establishes the starting point only (4.3× or 21.9× growth); the destination
   is set by physics and orientation and is unchanged either way.

3. **THE 0.4 mm STACK-UP CHECK (required before 0.4 mm is frozen, D4).** Whether a 0.4 mm plate of
   the ≈103 mm size survives the loads the four ≈176 mm cantilevered wings impose, and whether the

## 8. Appended 2026-10-07 — D4's 0.4 mm STIFFNESS CHECK has been RUN: **it cannot be settled from the record**

> **Appended, not a rewrite.** Nothing above this line is changed or deleted; the history stays
> legible. This section records the outcome of the check D4 itself demands and Open item 3
> carries. It **does not** change D4's decision (0.4 mm remains the target) and it **does not**
> close Open item 3, which is closed only **in part**.

**The check.** `docs/analysis/hub-thickness-deflection.md` (+ its committed model,
`docs/analysis/hub_thickness_deflection_model.py`) runs the closed-form plate / end-only-joint
analysis D4 asked for: the 103 × 103 mm plate, the four ~176 mm vertical-blade wings
(ADR-049 §5 item 6), the end-only unbonded cells (ADR-052), and the pad-to-tab socket joint
(ADR-049 §5 item 4, plain pads, no slot). **Verdict: `cannot be settled from the record`.**

**Why not — the record is missing every decisive input.** The check needs seven quantities and
the repo contains **none** of them: (1) the plate's **boundary condition** (mount count,
positions, standoff stiffness — only the v1 `docs/hardware-design.md` 158–165 prose exists, and
D6 leaves the standoff height `TODO(unverified)`); (2) FR4's **elastic modulus** (the repo's own
mass model carries FR4 *density* only); (3) the end-only solder joint's **allowable shear
strain**; (4) the cell's **float gap / fillet height**; (5) the wing **orientation**, which
ADR-048 `OPEN-23` still carries as an unresolved contradiction (it changes the governing root
moment **7×**); (6) any **rotation rate**; (7) any **launch/release/drop acceleration**. The
same arithmetic therefore yields **opposite verdicts** on the honest readings: 0.4 mm sits
**inside** a defensible joint budget under the global-plate model and **1.4–2.7× outside** it
under the local-socket model. **A single coupon bend test (named in the analysis, §13) collapses
the whole question to one number.**

**What the check DOES establish, and it is worth recording:**

1. **The 4.5 g prize is confirmed exactly: 4.514 g** (13.542 g → 9.028 g at 106.1 cm²) — D4's
   figure and §1.5's arithmetic both check out.
2. **The lever's price is exact and assumption-free: 3.375× the deflection and 2.250× the plate
   surface strain** at equal load (`δ ∝ 1/t³`, `ε ∝ 1/t²`).
3. **Thermal is NOT a differentiator.** The FR4↔Si CTE-mismatch term (752–1232 µε over a
   78.55 mm cell) is **independent of plate thickness**; cold *helps* the plate's stiffness.
   ADR-052's end-only mount is what makes it survivable, and that is already decided.
4. **The binding question is LOCAL, not global.** The wing's root moment enters through a 9 mm
   tab at the plate edge, so the socket land row — not the plate's mid-span — is where the
   thinned plate bites. Global plate deflection at 0.4 mm computes to **0.34 mm (0.33 % of
   span)**, i.e. smaller than ordinary bare-FR4 panel warpage; the *local* socket surface
   strain doubles from **152 µε (0.6 mm) to 343 µε (0.4 mm)**.
5. **A local stiffener (4 glued 0.4 mm doublers over the socket land rows, ≈0.613 g) recovers
   MORE socket margin than 0.6 mm has** (86 µε vs 152 µε) while still banking **3.90 g** — but
   it is **4 added hand joints** on a hand-soldered, minimum-part-count payload (ADR-049/ADR-052
   doctrine) and it does **not** help the global case. **If the question is settled
   mechanically, the likely answer is "adequate only with local stiffening" — a ~0.6 g, four-
   joint cost, NOT a free 4.5 g.**
6. **Live inconsistency found while reading the inputs, recorded so it is not frozen blindly:**
   ADR-051 §2.2 recommends H2 = 2 LARGE cells (61.2 cm²) while this record's D5 sizes the plate
   at 106.1 cm², which needs ≥4 LARGE cells (3 × 30.6 = 91.8 < 106.1). D3's "ceiling" framing
   means they are not strictly contradictory, but the **cell count on the plate is unsettled**
   (~3 g of on-plate mass). It does not change the stiffness verdict.

**Consequence for this record's own text.**

- **D4 stands unchanged.** 0.4 mm is still the target; the check found **no evidence it is
  inadequate for the global plate load**, and it found the *reason* the thickness lever is
  cheap-or-expensive is a **local socket detail**, not the plate size.
- **Open item 3 is now PARTLY closed.** Its *mechanical* half has been analysed and **answered
  as a named gap with a specified bench test**, not with an invented modulus (per the repo's
  own honesty rule, and per D4's own words about the check being required). Its *stack-up* half
  — whether a 0.4 mm layer count is fabricable on the chosen supplier — **remains open and is a
  supplier question**.
- **D4's "The stiffness trade is noted" sentence is superseded in form, not in direction:** the
  trade is no longer a bare note; it is quantified in `docs/analysis/hub-thickness-deflection.md`
  with the verdict `cannot be settled from the record` and a named measurement.
- **No fab, schematic, netlist, placement, BOM or order change is authorised by this section.
  Order nothing. No thickness is frozen.**

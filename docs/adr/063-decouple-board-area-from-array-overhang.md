## 0. The decision, in one paragraph

**The hub board is sized by its COMPONENTS, not by its array. Panels may overhang it.** ADR-055 D5
sized the hub at **103 × 103 mm = 106.09 cm²** for exactly one reason — so that a 106.1 cm²
horizontal-day-mean array would fit on it — and that is why a 55 × 45 mm outline was rejected
(`tracker/hardware/PLACEMENT-S0-FREEZE-v9-hub.md` §3). The operator has now removed that
requirement: **board area and array area are two independent numbers.** The outline follows the
**measured component courtyard (2582 mm², ≈ 60 × 60 mm)**; the array is carried by a **separate
rib/strip carrier** that overhangs the board, **at a pitch set by a bench measurement of the
0.21 mm cell's unsupported span — a number this record does NOT freeze, because it is not in the
record.**

---

## 1. Context

### 1.1 The coupling this record breaks, quoted

ADR-055 **D5**: *"Arithmetic: 106.1 cm² implies a continuous single-face square of
`√106.1 cm² ≈ 103 mm`, i.e. ≈ 103 × 103 mm."* ADR-051 **§2.3**: *"The hub outline grows from
22 × 22 mm to **a panel sized by the array** … This is a **FORM-FACTOR CHANGE**, not a tweak."*

That is the coupling: **outline = f(array)**. It is the reason the 55 × 45 mm board — which the
placement work then showed was **too small for the components alone** — was not the answer.

### 1.2 The coupling was also arithmetically unsatisfiable

**The board ADR-055 D5 chose could not hold the cells D5 sized it for.** A LARGE cell is
**78.55 × 38.90 mm** (ADR-049 Measured inputs). In a 103 × 103 mm square:

```
at most floor(103 / 38.90) = 2 cells stack on their 38.90 mm side;
2 stacked cells occupy 78.55 x 77.80 mm, leaving 25.20 mm of height — the cell's
  smallest side (38.90) does not fit -> a 3rd cell is impossible
=> maximum 2 LARGE cells = 2 x 30.5559 = 61.11 cm^2 = 58 % of the 106.1 cm^2 target
```

while the **area minimum for 106.1 cm² is 4 LARGE cells** (3 × 30.6 = 91.7 < 106.1), and 4 cells
need a 2 × 2 block of **157.1 × 77.8 mm** — **54 mm wider than the board**. (SMALL cells fare no
better: 8 tile in the same square = 81.85 cm² = 77 % of target.)
**So the 106.1 cm² "full duty" figure was never realisable on the 103 mm board D5 specified.**
The coupling was not merely inconvenient; it was **inconsistent**, and the operator's decision is
what resolves it. (Full arithmetic: analysis §2.)

### 1.3 The risk the decision relocates, not removes

The cells are **0.21 mm bare silicon mounted END-ONLY with NO adhesive bond** (ADR-052 §2.1). On a
board, **the board is the cell's support**. If a cell overhangs the board, **the board is no longer
supporting it across the overhanging span**: something else must carry it, or the cell flexes and
cracks. **The operator's decision relocates support mass; it does not delete it.** ADR-049 already
flags the governing question as open: *"whether spine-and-ribs leaves unsupported silicon spans
that crack; **the frame's rib pitch is set by that answer**"* (ADR-049 §Open items). The analysis
quantifies the demand (§3 below) and names the measurement (§4) — but the allowable is **not in the
record**, so **this record does not freeze a pitch.**

## 2. Decision

### D1. **Board area and array area are DECOUPLED.** The hub outline is component-driven; panels may overhang.

**The hub board is sized by its components and its attachment lands. It is NOT sized by the array,
and the array is permitted to extend beyond it.**

The component floor is **measured, not assumed** — `PLACEMENT-S0-FREEZE-v9-hub.md` §3:

```
39 footprinted components demand 2582 mm^2 (25.82 cm^2) of courtyard
55 x 45 mm board = 2475 mm^2 (usable interior 2279 mm^2)
interior demand 2203 mm^2 into 2279 mm^2 = 96.7 % packing -> the pack left 28 of 39
   parts unseated and scored 389 pad overlaps / 199 courtyard overlaps
```

At the realistic packing band (72–80 %; the 96.7 % demand **failed**):

```
25.82 cm^2 @ 72 % -> >= 35.86 cm^2 -> square 59.9 mm
25.82 cm^2 @ 75 % -> >= 34.43 cm^2 -> square 58.7 mm
25.82 cm^2 @ 80 % -> >= 32.27 cm^2 -> square 56.8 mm
plus the four socket land rows 4 x 8 x 4.0 x 1.2 mm = 154 mm^2 + their 1.0-5.0 mm keep-out band
```

**Resulting minimum outline: ≈ 60 × 60 mm (36.0 cm²)** — **≈ 34 % of the 103 × 103 mm board**, a
**66 % area reduction**, saving **5.96 g** of 0.4 mm FR4 (9.028 g → 3.064 g). **Square is retained**
because ADR-055 D5's square preference rests on the four socket interfaces at 90° (ADR-046/048),
which the hub still has. The exact outline is a **placement-stage floorplan**, frozen only after
the placement is re-run on it (**D5**).

### D2. **The 103 × 103 mm outline of ADR-055 D5 is RETIRED, and the existing placement is PROVISIONAL.**

`tracker/hardware/hub_board_v9.kicad_pcb` (the first v9 hub placement, S0 PASS) sits on the retired
103 mm outline. Its **component-courtyard measurement is the floor this record uses**, but its
**outline, seats and placement hash are not a design of record** and **must be re-placed on the
new outline** once D4's measurement lands.

**This record does not re-place anything, and no board file is touched.** The outline moves
**after** the support question closes; re-placing onto a moving target is the waste this record
exists to avoid. `hub_board_v9.kicad_pcb` is **unmodified**.

### D3. **The array is carried by a SEPARATE, overhanging rib/strip carrier — not by the hub board.**

Because the array is no longer confined to the board (D1), it needs a support of its own:

- **Recommended arrangement (from the analysis §6.2):** the **spine-and-ribs pattern of ADR-049**,
  applied to the hub array, **unbonded** (ADR-052 §2.1), with the cells **overhanging on two
  opposite edges** — not all four.
- **Why two opposite edges, not all four:** with end-only lands each cell must have **both** ends on
  a support land. Overhanging on **two opposite** edges keeps one axis of the array on the board's
  own structure and puts only the far end on a rib strip. Overhanging on **all four** leaves
  **both** ends of the outer cells uncarried — the **cantilever** case (analysis §3.2: **4 × the
  surface stress, 9.6 × the sag** of the simply-supported case) — and is the highest-crack-risk,
  lowest-saving arrangement. **It is not recommended.**
- **Mass (analysis §6.1/§6.3), for a 122.2 cm² array of 4 LARGE cells (5.980 g of Si):**

```
(a)  full carrier 0.6 mm, board = array footprint : 19.523 g
(a') full carrier 0.4 mm                          : 15.009 g
(b)  skeletonised carrier (28.4 % board)          : 10.408 g (0.6 mm) / 8.932 g (0.4 mm)
(c)  spine + ribs (measured wing fraction, hub-scaled): 12.460 g   <- RECOMMENDED
(d)  cells + ribbon harness + separate elec. board: 10.904 g
```

The spine-and-ribs hub carrier is **12.460 g** (board 6.480 g + cells 5.980 g) against **19.523 g**
for the 0.6 mm full carrier — a **7.06 g** saving, of the same class as the **16.35 g** the same
change banked for the wing (ADR-052 §2.6).

### D4. **OPEN — the carrier's PITCH, the maximum overhang and the achievable array area are NOT frozen. `TODO(unverified)`.**

**This record does NOT set a rib pitch, a maximum overhang length or an array-area ceiling, because
the deciding number is not in the record.** There is **no sourceable allowable** for the unsupported
span of a 0.21 mm cell: the repo carries only the cell's **geometry**, its **areal mass**
(48.93 mg/cm²) and **one elastic constant** (Si modulus 170 GPa, carried as "standard" in
`docs/analysis/hub-thickness-deflection.md` §3). **No flexural strength or modulus of rupture is in
the repo or in any datasheet the operator has supplied.** Per this repo's honesty rule —
*"a precise-looking number built on an invented modulus is worse than a named gap"* — the demand is
stated instead:

```
simply-supported span L, self-weight:  sigma = 3 rho g L^2 / (4 t) ;  delta = 5 rho g L^4 / (32 E t^2)
  LARGE cell 78.55 mm @ 1 g : sigma = 0.504 MPa, delta = 18.1 um
  LARGE cell 78.55 mm @ 2 g : sigma = 1.007 MPa, delta = 36.2 um
  cantilever (one end uncarried, 78.55 mm) @ 1 g : sigma = 2.01 MPa, delta = 174 um
```

**What closes D4 — one bench measurement (the operator's own work, ADR-052 §2.7):**

> Support one real 78.55 × 38.90 × 0.21 mm cell at its two ends over a gap `S`, end-only, no bond.
> Load to 1 g / 2 g and **increase `S` until the cell cracks. Record `S_crack`.** Repeat
> cold-soaked at ≈ −55 °C. **`pitch ≤ S_crack`** (with margin for the unbounded pre-launch drop
> case); the same run sets the maximum overhang and, through `S_crack`, the array-area ceiling.

**Until `S_crack` is measured, no number in D4 is frozen, and no pitch may be laid out from this
record.** (The demand table above is the metric `S_crack` converts into.)

### D5. **The outline is set only after the placement is re-run — and the placement gate (ADR-030) governs.**

When D4's measurement lands, the **≈ 60 × 60 mm** component-driven outline (D1) is re-placed
(ADR-030's deterministic placement gate, hash re-frozen), and **only then** is the outline a fab
candidate. Until then the outline is a **floorplan**, and the existing 103 mm placement is
**provisional** (D2).

### D6. **The duty margin overhang buys is CONDITIONAL on the charge path.**

Overhang lets the array exceed 106.1 cm² (analysis §4: 4 LARGE = 122.2 cm² = **115 %** of full duty;
6 LARGE = 183.3 cm² = **173 %**) — i.e. it buys **duty margin, not only board saving**. **But
ADR-051 §2.2 already records that a 61.2 cm² face-on array exceeds the bq25570 harvester's 510 mW
absolute-maximum input by 2.26×**, and growing the array makes that worse. **The extra duty is
usable only with a higher-input-power harvester (LTC3105/SPV1040 class, ADR-051 §2.2
`TODO(unverified)`) or deliberate sub-MPP operation.** This record records the margin; **it does not
assert it is harvestable.**

## 4. Consequences

### Positive

- **The outline stops being hostage to the array.** The board shrinks to the **measured** component
  floor (25.82 cm²) — a **66 % area reduction** and **≈ 6 g** of FR4 saved — and the array is free
  to be whatever the duty and the support allow.
- **The internal inconsistency is exposed and cleared.** ADR-055 D5's 103 mm board could not hold
  the ≥ 4 LARGE cells its 106.1 cm² target needs (§1.2); the decoupling removes a target that was
  never reachable as specified.
- **The price-tier cliff is crossed.** The measured boundary is a **largest side ≤ 102 mm**
  (analysis §5.3: 103 mm 4-layer = **$31.60** vs 102 mm = **$8.00**). A 60–70 mm outline is far
  inside the cheap class; the 103 mm board sat just over it.
- **The support question is turned into a measurable design variable.** The crack risk becomes a
  **pitch** set by one coupon test (D4), not an unanswerable yes/no about the board.

### Costs (accepted, and stated)

- **Support mass is relocated, not deleted.** A separate rib/strip carrier (~6.5 g of frame) plus a
  separate electronics board replace the single 106 cm² plate. The saving is real but it is a
  **different part count** — one more structure on a hand-soldered, minimum-part-count payload
  (ADR-049/ADR-052 doctrine).
- **A new failure mode is introduced: the carrier-to-board attachment**, which the single plate did
  not have.
- **The rib pitch is unresolved**, so the arrangement cannot be laid out yet; the array's maximum
  size is bounded by an **unmeasured** quantity (`TODO(unverified)`, D4).
- **The extra duty is conditional on the charge path** (D6); an array > 61.2 cm² face-on is outside
  the named harvester's rating.
- **The existing placement is invalidated** (D2) — the re-place cost is real and is accepted as the
  price of not re-placing onto a moving target.
